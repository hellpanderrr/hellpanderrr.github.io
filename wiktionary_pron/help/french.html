About This Tool

This French transcription app uses the Wiktionary French Pronunciation Module to generate phonemic transcriptions for French text. IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) is a standardized system for transcribing speech sounds using symbols based on the Latin alphabet. The tool converts French orthography into IPA. A phonemic transcription represents the abstract sound system of the language (phonemes), as opposed to the actual speech variants (allophones).

The system uses comprehensive Wiktionary data dumps[1][2] as a lexicon to first retrieve transcriptions from the dictionary. When a word is not found in the lexicon, it falls back to generating transcriptions with the rules of the pronunciation module.

The text is transcribed word by word: the converter splits the input at spaces and transcribes each word on its own. Liaison and elision between separate words are therefore not applied (see Apostrophes, Hyphens and Liaison).

Dialects & Limitations

  • Supported: two style options for Metropolitan French, Default and Parisian (experimental), described below.
  • Not supported: separate profiles for other varieties of French (Quebec, Belgian, Swiss, southern French and others).
  • Edge cases: loanwords, proper names, and words whose pronunciation is an exception to the spelling rules may be transcribed incorrectly when they are not in the lexicon (see Common Issues & Limitations).
  • Technical limit: each word is processed independently, and characters other than letters, the apostrophe, the hyphen and the liaison mark ‿ are removed from the input before transcription.

Available Style Profiles

  • Default - conservative standard French

    This option keeps all four nasal vowels distinct, a contrast that is retained in some varieties of French, for example Quebec and Belgian French, and is common in dictionary transcriptions.

    • brun is pronounced /bʁœ̃/ /bʁœ̃/
    • brin is pronounced /bʁɛ̃/ /bʁɛ̃/
    • blanc is pronounced /blɑ̃/ /blɑ̃/
    • bon is pronounced /bɔ̃/ /bɔ̃/
  • Parisian (experimental) - Parisian nasal-vowel pattern

    This option rewrites the nasal vowels of the Default result so that the four-vowel system becomes a three-vowel system. It is a fixed substitution on the transcription, not a separate pronunciation module, and it changes nothing but nasal vowels. The substitutions run in this order:

    • The "un/in" merger: /œ̃/ (in brun) and /ɛ̃/ (in brin) both become /ɑ̃/.
      • brun: /bʁœ̃/ → /bʁɑ̃/
      • brin: /bʁɛ̃/ → /bʁɑ̃/
      • Result: brun and brin become homophones.
    • The "an/on" chain shift: This is a two-step shift, not a merger.
      1. First, /ɔ̃/ (as in bon) is replaced by /õ/ (the letter o with a tilde).
      2. Then /ɑ̃/ (as in blanc) is replaced by /ɔ̃/. Because this step runs after the first one, the /ɔ̃/ that it produces is not changed again.
      • bon: /bɔ̃/ → /bõ/
      • blanc: /blɑ̃/ → /blɔ̃/
      • Result: bon and blanc remain distinct.

    The substitution is applied to the Default result whether it came from the lexicon or from the rules. No other Parisian feature is modelled.

General Limitations

  • No liaison, elision or h aspiré handling between separate words (each word is transcribed alone).
  • The rules know no exceptions of their own beyond those listed under Implementation Details; irregular words are correct only when the lexicon contains them.
  • Digits, punctuation and other symbols are discarded.

Phonemic vs Phonetic

  • Phonemic (with dictionary support): A simplified, broad transcription that shows only the sounds that are essential for distinguishing meaning (phonemes). It represents the abstract sound system of the language. Uses the Wiktionary lexicon when the word is in it and falls back to the rules otherwise.
  • Phonetic: not available for French. Only the Phonemic form exists.

Quick Reference (Glossary)

Phoneme
The smallest unit of sound in a language that distinguishes meaning (e.g., /p/ vs. /b/ in English "pat" vs. "bat").
Allophone
A variant pronunciation of a phoneme that doesn't change meaning (e.g., aspirated [pʰ] vs. unaspirated [p]).
Nasal Vowel
A vowel pronounced with air passing through the nose, written with a combining tilde: pain /pɛ̃/. French has four: /ɑ̃/, /ɛ̃/, /ɔ̃/, /œ̃/.
Schwa (Mute E)
The reduced vowel ə, written e: le /lə/. In many positions it is not pronounced: samedi /sam.di/.
Optional Schwa
A schwa shown in parentheses, (ə), may be pronounced or dropped: porte-monnaie /pɔʁ.t(ə).mɔ.nɛ/.
Syllable Boundary
A dot (.) showing where syllables divide.
Liaison
The pronunciation of a normally silent final consonant before a following vowel. The module marks the junction with the undertie ‿.
Elision
The loss of a final vowel before a vowel, written with an apostrophe: l'arbre /l‿aʁbʁ/.
Aspirated H (h aspiré)
An initial h that behaves like a consonant and blocks liaison and elision. The rules treat every h as silent: homme /ɔm/, haricot /a.ʁi.ko/.
Glide (Semivowel)
A consonant-like vowel sound: /j/ in pied /pje/, /w/ in oui /wi/, /ɥ/ in nuit /nɥi/.
Hiatus
Two vowels in adjacent syllables with no glide between them: théâtre /te.atʁ/.
Diaeresis (tréma)
The two dots in ë, ï, ü, marking a vowel that is pronounced separately from the preceding one: maïs /ma.i/.
Uvular R
The French r, /ʁ/, produced at the back of the mouth, not with the tip of the tongue as in English.
Silent Letter
A letter that is written but not pronounced, most often a word-final consonant.
Geminate
A doubled consonant letter. In French it is read as a single consonant: terre /tɛʁ/.
Devoicing
The change of a voiced consonant to a voiceless one. The module writes b as p before s or t: absurde /ap.syʁd/.
Assibilation
The change of t to s before i and a vowel in some endings: nation /na.sjɔ̃/. It is blocked after s: question /kɛs.tjɔ̃/.
Voiced and Voiceless S
Between vowels a single s is voiced, a double ss is voiceless: poison /pwa.zɔ̃/, poisson /pwa.sɔ̃/.
Open and Closed Vowels
French has paired mid vowels, closed /o/, /ø/ and open /ɔ/, /œ/, whose choice depends on the position in the word: jeu /ʒø/, peur /pœʁ/.
Ligature œ
The written combination of o and e, read as the vowel of cœur /kœʁ/.
Respelling
Typing a different spelling to obtain a different transcription, for example vile /vil/ for ville. See Common Issues & Limitations.
Lexicon and Rules
The lexicon is the stored Wiktionary transcription of a word. The rules are the spelling-to-sound algorithm of the pronunciation module, used when the word is not in the lexicon.

Interactive Features

  • Click words to cycle variants: Some words have more than one transcription in the lexicon. Click a transcribed word to see the alternatives.
  • Audio playback: Click the speaker icon next to any word or line to hear text-to-speech pronunciation (requires browser TTS support).
  • Export results: Use the PDF or CSV buttons to save transcriptions.

Multiple Pronunciation Variants

  • Lexicon entries: A lexicon entry may hold several readings. The first is displayed and a click cycles through the others.
  • Rule output: The module itself can generate alternatives (for example /pa/ and /pɑ/ for pas; /sɔ.sja.lism/ and /sɔ.sja.lizm/ for socialisme; /tjɛ.nɑ̃/ and /tje.nɑ̃/ for tiennent), but the app displays only the first one.
  • Lexicon vs. rules: Dictionary lookup and rule-based generation may produce different results for the same word.

French Pronunciation Guide

This guide describes what the module does with a word typed in ordinary spelling. The examples were generated by the rules alone; in the app a word that is in the lexicon shows its Wiktionary transcription instead, which may differ.

Oral Vowels and Accents

Accents

  • é is /e/: été /e.te/, musée /my.ze/
  • è, ê are /ɛ/: père /pɛʁ/, tête /tɛt/
  • à, ù, î do not change the vowel: là /la/, où /u/
  • â is the back vowel /ɑ/ in the second variant only, so the app shows /a/: âme /am/
  • ô is always /o/: hôtel /o.tɛl/, rôle /ʁol/
  • û is /y/: mûr /myʁ/ (jeûne /ʒøn/ has /ø/)
  • ë, ï, ü mark a separate vowel; the dots are then dropped: Noël /nɔ.ɛl/, naïf /na.if/

The Letter E

  • e before a doubled consonant is /ɛ/, but initial e + double consonant gives /e/: terre /tɛʁ/, serrer /sɛ.ʁe/, effacer /e.fa.se/
  • e before a final d, g, p, t (optionally + s) is /ɛ/: permet /pɛʁ.mɛ/, legs /lɛ/
  • ai, ei are /ɛ/: maison /mɛ.zɔ̃/, neige /nɛʒ/
  • -er, -ez, -es (short words) are /e/: parler /paʁ.le/, nez /ne/, les /le/; in a one-syllable word -er is /ɛʁ/: fer /fɛʁ/, mer /mɛʁ/

Vowel Digraphs

au, eau /o/ chaud /ʃo/, beau /bo/

Open and Closed O

The letter o is closed /o/ when it ends the word or precedes z, and open /ɔ/ elsewhere. The accent ô is always closed.

  • Closed: zéro /ze.ʁo/, chose /ʃoz/, trop /tʁo/
  • Open: porte /pɔʁt/, sol /sɔl/

Nasal Vowels

A vowel followed by n before a consonant or at the end of a word is nasalized and the n is not pronounced. A vowel followed by m is nasalized before b, p and f, and in final -om and -aim/-eim.

Spelling IPA Examples
an, am, en, em /ɑ̃/ blanc /blɑ̃/, champ /ʃɑ̃/, dent /dɑ̃/, ensemble /ɑ̃.sɑ̃bl/
in, im, yn, ym, ain, aim, ein /ɛ̃/ pain /pɛ̃/, faim /fɛ̃/, sympa /sɛ̃.pa/, syndicat /sɛ̃.di.ka/
on, om /ɔ̃/ bon /bɔ̃/, nom /nɔ̃/, plomb /plɔ̃/
un, um /œ̃/ brun /bʁœ̃/, lundi /lœ̃.di/

  • No nasalization before a vowel or before a doubled n or m: année /a.ne/, inutile /i.ny.til/, femme /fɛm/, bonne /bɔn/
  • Final -um is /ɔm/: maximum /mak.si.mɔm/, album /al.bɔm/
  • In the Parisian (experimental) style the nasal vowels are rewritten; see Dialects & Limitations.

Mute E (Schwa)

  • Final e after a consonant is dropped in a word of two or more syllables: porte /pɔʁt/, table /tabl/, femme /fɛm/, rose /ʁoz/
  • In a one-syllable word the schwa is kept: le /lə/, je /ʒə/, de /də/
  • In the first syllable the schwa is kept: petit /pə.ti/, venir /və.niʁ/, leçon /lə.sɔ̃/
  • Inside a word, between a vowel and a single consonant followed by another consonant, the schwa is dropped: samedi /sam.di/, médecin /med.sɛ̃/
  • The schwa is kept where dropping it would create a cluster that the module lists as impossible (ʁʁ, ɲʁ, ɲl): agnelet /a.ɲə.lɛ/, indignerez /ɛ̃.di.ɲə.ʁe/, déchirerez /de.ʃi.ʁə.ʁe/; and after a consonant plus ʁ: vendredi /vɑ̃.dʁə.di/
  • Undecided cases are shown as optional: porte-monnaie /pɔʁ.t(ə).mɔ.nɛ/

Consonant Rules

C and Ç

  • c is /s/ before e, i, y and /k/ elsewhere: cinéma /si.ne.ma/, cygne /siɲ/, coq /kɔk/
  • ç is always /s/: ça /sa/, garçon /ɡaʁ.sɔ̃/
  • œ before c does not soften it in cœur /kœʁ/

G, GU and GN

  • g is /ʒ/ before e, i, y: manger /mɑ̃.ʒe/, gymnase /ʒim.naz/; ge before a, o, u also gives /ʒ/: geai /ʒɛ/
  • g is /ɡ/ elsewhere: gare /ɡaʁ/, gorge /ɡɔʁʒ/
  • gu + vowel is /ɡ/: guerre /ɡɛʁ/, guitare /ɡi.taʁ/; with a diaeresis the u is kept: aigüe /ɛ.ɡy/
  • gn is /ɲ/: agneau /a.ɲo/, ignorer /i.ɲɔ.ʁe/

QU

qu is /k/: qui /ki/, quelque /kɛlk/, quoi /kwa/.

CH, PH and TH

  • ch is /ʃ/: chat /ʃa/, machine /ma.ʃin/; before r, l, n it is /k/: chrétien /kʁe.sjɛ̃/, chlore /klɔʁ/, technique /tɛk.nik/
  • ph is /f/: pharmacie /faʁ.ma.si/
  • th is read as t, because the h is silent: thé /te/

S and SS

  • Between vowels a single s is /z/: maison /mɛ.zɔ̃/, désert /de.zɛʁ/, poison /pwa.zɔ̃/
  • Elsewhere, and for ss, it is /s/: sauce /sos/, basse /bas/, poisson /pwa.sɔ̃/

X

  • ex before a vowel, and initial x, are /ɡz/: examen /ɛɡ.za.mɑ̃/, exact /ɛɡ.zakt/, xylophone /ɡzi.lɔ.fɔn/
  • Other x is /ks/: taxi /tak.si/, texte /tɛkst/, excellent /ɛk.sɛ.lɑ̃/

H

h is silent: homme /ɔm/, hôtel /o.tɛl/, bonheur /bɔ.nœʁ/. The rules do not distinguish h muet from h aspiré.

T Before I (Assibilation)

ti is /si/ before on, en, al, el when the t does not follow s, x or ç and does not start the word: nation /na.sjɔ̃/, action /ak.sjɔ̃/, patient /pa.sjɑ̃/, initial /i.ni.sjal/. After s the t stays: question /kɛs.tjɔ̃/. Other ti is /tj/ or /ti/: amitié /a.mi.tje/, entier /ɑ̃.tje/.

B Before S or T

b before s or t is pronounced as voiceless /p/: absurde /ap.syʁd/, obtenir /ɔp.tə.niʁ/, subtil /syp.til/.

Doubled Consonants

A doubled consonant letter is read as one consonant: terre /tɛʁ/, pomme /pɔm/, bonne /bɔn/.

Glides, Y and ILL

I, U and OU Before a Vowel

  • i + vowel: /j/: pied /pje/, lieu /ljø/
  • u + vowel: /ɥ/: nuit /nɥi/, cuire /kɥiʁ/
  • ou + vowel: /w/: oui /wi/, jouer /ʒwe/
  • After a consonant plus l or r, i and ou stay full vowels, with a separate glide or vowel after them: crier /kʁi.je/, prier /pʁi.je/, brouette /bʁu.ɛt/. The glide /ɥ/ is kept before i: fruit /fʁɥi/, bruit /bʁɥi/

The Letter Y

  • Initial y before a vowel is /j/: yeux /jø/
  • Initial y before a consonant, or y between consonants, is /i/: yttrium /i.tʁi.jɔm/, cycle /sikl/
  • Y between vowels counts as i plus j: voyage /vwa.jaʒ/, crayon /kʁɛ.jɔ̃/, payer /pɛ.je/, joyeux /ʒwa.jø/
  • Final -ay is read as -ai: Gamay /ɡa.mɛ/

ILL and IL

  • ill after a consonant is /ij/: fille /fij/, famille /fa.mij/
  • ill after a vowel is /j/: travailler /tʁa.va.je/, bouteille /bu.tɛj/, pavillon /pa.vi.jɔ̃/
  • uill after a consonant: /ɥij/; cuillère /kɥi.jɛʁ/, juillet /ʒɥi.jɛ/
  • Final il after a vowel is /j/: soleil /sɔ.lɛj/, travail /tʁa.vaj/; after a consonant the l is pronounced: fil /fil/, avril /a.vʁil/

Hiatus

A diaeresis, or two vowels that no rule joins, gives two syllables: naïf /na.if/, haïr /a.iʁ/, théâtre /te.atʁ/, aïe /a.i/.

Apostrophes, Hyphens and Liaison

The converter transcribes each space-separated word on its own. Liaison, elision and h aspiré between words are therefore not applied: les and amis are transcribed independently. Only material inside one written word is joined.

Apostrophe

An apostrophe inside a word marks elision. It becomes the junction mark ‿ in the output, and a consonant that precedes it is kept:

  • l'arbre /l‿aʁbʁ/, l'homme /l‿ɔm/
  • c'est /s‿ɛ/, n'est /n‿ɛ/, s'il /s‿il/
  • qu'on /k‿ɔ̃/, jusqu'à /ʒys.k‿a/, aujourd'hui /o.ʒuʁ.d‿ɥi/

Hyphen

Each part of a hyphenated word is processed as a word of its own, so silent final letters of the first part are dropped: grand-père /ɡʁɑ̃.pɛʁ/, peut-être /pø.ɛtʁ/, porte-monnaie /pɔʁ.t(ə).mɔ.nɛ/.

Typed Liaison Mark

The input filter keeps the undertie ‿ (U+203F). If it is typed or pasted between two parts of a word, the module applies liaison to the first part. Results of the rules:

Input Consonant of liaison IPA
grand‿homme d is read /t/ /ɡʁɑ̃.t‿ɔm/
neuf‿heures f is read /v/ /nœ.v‿œʁ/
les‿amis, beaux‿arts s and x are read /z/ /le.z‿a.mi/, /bo.z‿aʁ/
y‿a a glide /j/ is inserted after i /i.j‿a/
mon‿ami, un‿ami nasal vowel kept, n pronounced /mɔ̃.n‿a.mi/, /œ̃.n‿a.mi/
bon‿ami vowel denasalized, n pronounced /bɔ.n‿a.mi/

The nasal vowel is kept in the words matched by mon, ton, son, on, en, bien, rien, un and similar endings, and dropped before a liaison n in other words (bon, certain).

Using the Tool: Practical Notes

Why does the output for a word differ from the rules described on this page?

The app looks a word up in the Wiktionary lexicon first and uses the rules of the pronunciation module only when the word is missing, so a dictionary word shows the Wiktionary transcription, including exceptions that the rules do not know. Open in the converter

What exactly does the Parisian (experimental) style change?

It is not a separate module but a post-processing step applied to the Default output, whether that output came from the lexicon or from the rules. Only nasal vowels are rewritten, in this order: /ɔ̃/ becomes /õ/, then /ɑ̃/ becomes /ɔ̃/, then /œ̃/ and /ɛ̃/ both become /ɑ̃/. Because the steps run in sequence, the /ɔ̃/ that results from /ɑ̃/ is not changed again. For maison the rules alone give /mɛ.zɔ̃/ in Default and /mɛ.zõ/ in Parisian. See Dialects Supported.

Is there a phonetic transcription?

No. Only the Phonemic form exists for French; see Transcription Forms.

Can the lexicon be switched off?

Not from the interface; the app has no setting for it. The lexicon is used by default, and opening the converter with dict=false in the address (for example ?lang=French&dict=false) turns it off, so that every word is transcribed by the rules. This is useful for seeing what the rules alone produce for a word.

Implementation Details (for Developers)

Processing in the App

  1. Input filter: The converter splits the text at spaces. Each word is then reduced to letters, combining marks, the apostrophe (the typographic ’ is converted to '), the hyphen and the mark ‿, and is normalized to NFKC. Digits and other symbols are discarded.
  2. Lexicon lookup: In the Phonemic form, if the lexicon french_lexicon_v3.zip is loaded, the word is looked up with every character except letters, marks and hyphens removed (so an apostrophe is removed from the key), first as typed and then in lower case. A hit is used as the result and may hold several readings.
  3. Rule generation: If there is no hit, the result is the first alternative returned by fr_ipa.show(word) of the Wiktionary module. No part-of-speech argument is passed.
  4. Parisian (experimental) post-processing: if that style is selected and a result exists, the string is rewritten as described below.

The lexicon is on by default. Opening the converter with dict=false in the address turns it off and leaves only step 3. There is no interface setting for this.

Parisian Post-processing

Three replacements run in sequence on the whole result string:

  1. ɔ̃ is replaced by õ (o with tilde).
  2. ɑ̃ is replaced by ɔ̃.
  3. œ̃ and ɛ̃ are replaced by ɑ̃.

Because of the order, the ɔ̃ created in step 2 is not changed by step 1, and the ɑ̃ created in step 3 is not changed by step 2. Example: lundi goes /lœ̃.di/ to /lɑ̃.di/.

Module Pipeline (fr-pron)

The module works on one string with string-replacement rules. The stages below follow the order in the code.

1. Initialization and Normalization

  • Lower case and boundary markers: The word is lower-cased and the symbol ⁀ is added at its start and end and around hyphens (e.g., ⁀mot⁀). Markers are removed at the end.
  • Ligatures: œu and oeu become a placeholder Eu; œil becomes Euil; any other œ becomes æ.
    Why? The capital E does not trigger the softening of c. cœur keeps a hard /k/: cœur /kœʁ/. A plain oeil is not covered: oeil /ɔ.ɛj/ (see Common Issues).

2. Soft C and G

  • Soft c: c + e, i, y, é, è, ê, æ → ç (later /s/).
  • Soft g: g + e, i, y → j (later /ʒ/).
  • Forced soft g: ge + a, o, u (and accented forms) → j; the e is orthographic and is removed (geai /ʒɛ/).

3. Verb Overrides (only with pos="v")

When the module is called with the part of speech v it applies verb rules: final ai becomes é; ti before ons/ens and t before ienne(nt) are protected from assibilation; and final -ent becomes silent e (or ət before a liaison mark).

Not used by the app. The app calls the module without a part of speech, so these rules do not run: the app gives parlent /paʁ.lɑ̃/ and portions /pɔʁ.sjɔ̃/. Called with pos="v" the module returns /paʁl/ and /pɔʁ.tjɔ̃/ (checked in the test harness).

4. Orthographic Substitutions

  • Accents: à, ù, î lose the accent; û becomes u, and eû becomes ø. â, ô, ê are kept because they change the vowel.
  • Final as, az: as, az → â (second variant /ɑ/; pas /pa/, gaz /ɡa/).
  • Devoicing: b before s or t (not at a word end) → p: absurde /ap.syʁd/, obtenir /ɔp.tə.niʁ/.
  • Digraphs: ph → f; gn → ɲ; compt → cont; psych → psik; ch before r, l, n → c (later /k/): chlore /klɔʁ/, chrétien /kʁe.sjɛ̃/.
  • aur: aur not preceded by e → or: dinosaure /di.no.zɔʁ/, but Beauregard /boʁ.ɡaʁ/.
  • Word-initial patterns:
    Pattern Logic Example
    eu- / neur- /ø/ even before r. Europe /ø.ʁɔp/
    hyper- / super- er is /ɛʁ/ (otherwise /e/). supermarché /sy.pɛʁ.maʁ.ʃe/
    trans- / intrans- + vowel s is voiced /z/. intransigeant /ɛ̃.tʁɑ̃.zi.ʒɑ̃/
    désh- s is voiced /z/. déshabiller /de.za.bi.je/
    et, es, est (whole words) /e/, /ɛ/, /ɛ/ (final t, s are then silent). est /ɛ/
  • Endings: -emment → -amment (évidemment /e.vi.da.mɑ̃/); -ied(s) → -ié (pied /pje/); e before a final d, g, p, t (+ s) → è (permet /pɛʁ.mɛ/); -ez → -éz; -er → -ér (premier /pʁə.mje/) but -èr in a one-syllable word or before a liaison mark (fer /fɛʁ/); ien at the end of a word → iɛn (bien /bjɛ̃/).

5. S, C, G, Q and T Handling

  • s between vowels → z (maison /mɛ.zɔ̃/).
  • qu → k (qu' → k', preventing false nasalization in qu'on /k‿ɔ̃/).
  • gu + vowel → g; with a diaeresis the u is kept (aigüe /ɛ.ɡy/).
  • -ing after a consonant → iŋ (parking /paʁ.kiŋ/); ngt → nt (vingt /vɛ̃/).
  • j → ʒ; sh, sch, ch → ʃ; remaining c, q → k.
  • Assibilation: ti + on, en and ti + al, el become si. It is blocked when the t follows s, ç, x or starts the word: nation /na.sjɔ̃/, question /kɛs.tjɔ̃/.

6. ILL, IL and Related Sequences

The rules run in this order:

  1. oel, oêl and oil (not before another l) → wal: poil /pwal/.
  2. Consonant + uill → ɥij: cuillère /kɥi.jɛʁ/.
  3. Vowel + ill → j: travailler /tʁa.va.je/.
  4. Any other ill not at the start of a word → ij: fille /fij/.
  5. Final or pre-consonant vowel + il → j: soleil /sɔ.lɛj/. After a consonant il keeps the l: fil /fil/.

7. The Letter Y

  • Final ay → ai (Gamay /ɡa.mɛ/); éy → éj (used in respellings).
  • Vowel + y → vowel + iy, so that voyage, crayon get /j/: crayon /kʁɛ.jɔ̃/. yi + vowel → y.y.
  • Between consonants, before a final e, and at the start of a word before a consonant, y → i: cycle /sikl/, yttrium /i.tʁi.jɔm/.
  • Consonant + y + vowel → i (later a glide where the rules allow); every other y becomes j (yeux /jø/).

8. Liaison Preparation

These rules matter only where a liaison mark ‿ is present, from the input or from an apostrophe.

  • d‿ → t‿; s‿, x‿ → z‿; f‿ → v‿; i‿ → ij‿.
  • A liaison consonant that would normally be silent (b, d, g, k, p, s, t, x, z) is treated as if it stood outside the word, so the preceding letters still follow their final-position rules.
  • Nasal bridge: n‿ after on at the start of a word (mon, ton, son, on), after 'on, and after en, ɛn, un is marked so that the vowel stays nasal and the n is pronounced (mon‿ami /mɔ̃.n‿a.mi/). Any other n‿ is marked so that the vowel stays oral (bon‿ami /bɔ.n‿a.mi/).

9. Word Endings (Deletions) and X

  • Silent letters: final s, z are deleted first, then x after i, u, ø (prix /pʁi/); nc(t) → n (instinct /ɛ̃s.tɛ̃/); final kt, st keep the t (ouest /wɛst/); ér → é; final mb, mp keep a placeholder that lets the vowel nasalize and are then removed (plomb /plɔ̃/); finally d, g, p, t are deleted.
  • Final e: deleted after a doubled consonant, after a consonant cluster starting with m or n, and after a vowel with at most one consonant. After n or m following a vowel it is replaced by a marker so that the consonant is pronounced (bonne /bɔn/, femme /fɛm/).
  • ç → s (after the final-s deletion, so ç can force an s).
  • x: (h)ex- before a vowel, in- + ex-, and initial x → gz; every other x → ks. examen /ɛɡ.za.mɑ̃/, xylophone /ɡzi.lɔ.fɔn/, taxi /tak.si/.

10. Double Consonants

  • Initial enn-, emm- + vowel → en_ (keeps the nasal).
  • Initial e + doubled consonant → é: effacer /e.fa.se/.
  • Other e + doubled consonant → è: terre /tɛʁ/.
  • Any remaining doubled consonant becomes single.

11. Vowel Digraphs

  • ou → U (later /u/); au, eau → O (later closed /o/).
  • eu, œu → ø before z or t, before a final h or at the end of a word; œ elsewhere.
  • ai, ei → ɛ.

12. Nasalization, OI and Silent H

  • nh → n, except initial enh- (inhérent keeps the n before the vowel: /i.ne.ʁɑ̃/).
  • Nasalization: a vowel plus n before a non-vowel, plus m before b, p, f, or final om, ɛm, is nasalized. The vowel maps as follows: a, e → /ɑ̃/; ɛ, i → /ɛ̃/ (a y has already become i); o → /ɔ̃/; u, ø, œ → /œ̃/.
  • The oin exception: oi + n/m: /wɛ̃/, as in point /pwɛ̃/, not /pwɑ̃/.
  • um before a non-vowel → ɔm: maximum /mak.si.mɔm/.
  • oi → wA (/wa/), done after nasalization so that noi sequences are not nasalized.
  • h is deleted, except after i or u, where an underscore keeps the vowel from becoming a glide.

13. Syllabification

  • V.CV: break before a single consonant.
  • VC.CV: break between two consonants.
  • Onset maximization: a consonant pair stays together as an onset when the second is l or r and the first is one of b, k, d, f, g, p, s, t, v; or when the pair is dʒ, or a consonant plus j, w, ɥ (tableau /ta.blo/). A break is restored between consonants that cannot start a syllable, except s before p, t, k (esprit /ɛs.pʁi/).
  • Diaeresis vowels (ä, ë, ï, ö, ü, ÿ) are converted to plain vowels after this step.

14. Single Vowels and Consonants

  • â → ɑ; e at the end of a word, or at the end of a syllable before a single consonant and a vowel, becomes ə; remaining e, è, ê, æ → ɛ; é → e.
  • Aperture of o: o at the end of a word, or before z, is closed (O); other o is ɔ; ô is o. u → y.
  • r → ʁ; g → ɡ (IPA g, U+0261).

15. Schwa Deletion

  • Final schwa: deleted in the last word of a polysyllabic word, before a following vowel (also across a liaison mark), and after a vowel. One-syllable words keep it.
  • Internal VCəCV: deleted when neither vowel is a schwa, unless the consonant pair is ʁʁ, ɲʁ or ɲl. A schwa before a consonant plus l or ʁ followed by a schwa is also deleted (palefrenier, vilebrequin).
  • Optional schwa: in other VCəCV sequences the schwa is shown as (ə), except between consonants that are the same apart from voicing; after two consonants a final schwa is kept as ə after an obstruent + approximant or m/n/l + r, and otherwise written (ə).

16. Glides and Reversion

  • Working from right to left: i + vowel → /j/; ou + vowel → /w/; u + vowel → /ɥ/.
  • Reversion: after a consonant plus l or ʁ, the glide from i or ou is replaced by a full vowel (brouette /bʁu.ɛt/ instead of /bʁwɛt/; crier /kʁi.je/). ɥ reverts to y only before a vowel other than i (fruit /fʁɥi/).

17. Final Cleanup and Alternatives

  • Apostrophes are rewritten as ‿, hyphens and the ⁀ markers are removed, and temporary capital letters are lower-cased.
  • Alternatives: the module returns additional readings in these cases: e/ɛ harmonized before a syllable boundary; -ism as -izm; and ɑ as a (the a form is listed first). The app takes the first reading only.

Respelling

A transcription can be changed by typing a different spelling, as in the table. Only ordinary letters, the accents, the apostrophe, the hyphen and ‿ survive the input filter, so the bracket and underscore notations of the Wiktionary template are not available. Example of a typed liaison mark: see Apostrophes, Hyphens and Liaison.

Limitations

  • Liaison, elision and h aspiré between separate words are not applied, because each word is transcribed alone.
  • Only the first reading generated by the module is shown.
  • Words with irregular pronunciation are correct only when they are in the lexicon.
  • There are no stress marks or length marks.
