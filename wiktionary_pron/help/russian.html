About This Tool

This Russian transcription app uses the Wiktionary Russian Pronunciation Module (ru-pron, the version maintained by Benwing2, with its helpers ru-translit and ru-common) to generate phonetic transcriptions for Russian text. IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) is a standardized system for transcribing speech sounds using symbols based on the Latin alphabet. This tool converts Russian orthography (spelling) into IPA, helping learners, linguists, and developers understand pronunciation.

Russian spelling does not show stress, and stress decides how most vowels sound. The module therefore needs to know which vowel is stressed before it can transcribe a word. The app obtains the stress in two ways:

  1. Lexicon first. A lexicon built from Wiktionary data dumps (about 500,000 entries) stores the position of the stressed vowel of each word form. If your word is found, the stress-marked form (for example вода́) is passed to the module. Homographs with different stress (for example за́мок / замо́к) are stored as several comma-separated forms, and each one is transcribed.
  2. Fallback for unknown words. If the word is not in the lexicon, the app transcribes it once for every possible stress position (one acute accent on each vowel in turn) and joins the results, for example /a/,/b/,/c/. Click the word to cycle through the candidates. A word with zero or one vowel is transcribed directly, because its stress is not in doubt.

You can also force the stress yourself by typing a combining acute accent (U+0301) after a vowel, and the letter ё is always treated as stressed when no other stress mark is present (see Stress Marks, Monosyllables and ё). Note, however, the known bug that a word which already carries an accent mark currently also receives a second, automatic stress in the fallback path.

About the examples on this page. Every IPA string below was produced by running the real engine, not written by hand. Unless a table says otherwise, the examples show the rule-based path, fed with stress-marked words (го́род), which is exactly what the browser produces for words found in the lexicon. For those words the browser picks the correct stress automatically; the lexicon itself is not available in the offline tool used to generate these examples.

Dialects & Limitations

  • Supported: One style, Default: standard Russian (Moscow-based literary pronunciation), with vowel reduction (akanye / ikanye), palatalization and assimilation as described by the module.
  • Not supported: Regional or dialectal pronunciation (for example non-reducing okanye speech). Colloquial fast-speech reductions are not modeled either.
  • Edge cases: The module works from spelling only. Loanwords whose е is pronounced [ɛ] after a hard consonant (кафе́, отель), initialisms (США), a few words with irregular -чн- or -вств- clusters, and proper names may need a respelling (see Common Issues & Limitations).
  • Technical limit: The app handler transcribes one word at a time. Digits are dropped, and some module options (part of speech, gemination, zhpal) are not exposed by the app.

Phonemic vs Phonetic

  • Phonetic (the only form offered for Russian): A detailed, narrow transcription that shows subtle, non-distinctive variations in pronunciation (allophones): reduced vowels [ɐ ə ɪ ʊ], fronted vowels [æ ʉ] next to soft consonants, palatalization [ʲ], dark [ɫ], and optional segments in parentheses. It aims to represent how a word is actually said.
  • Phonemic (not offered): The Wiktionary module produces only phonetic output for Russian, so the app has no separate phonemic form.

Quick Reference (Glossary)

Allophone
A variant pronunciation of a phoneme that doesn't change meaning, such as the reduced vowels [ɐ], [ə], [ɪ] that stand in for unstressed /a/, /o/, /e/ and /i/.
Akanye
The reduction of unstressed а and о: to ɐ immediately before the stress and to ə elsewhere, e.g. молоко́ /məɫɐˈko/.
Ikanye
The reduction of unstressed е, и, я to ɪ, e.g. де́ти /ˈdʲetʲɪ/.
Pretonic
The syllable directly before the stressed syllable. In the module it is the only unstressed position where а/о reduce to [ɐ] rather than [ə].
Tertiary Stress
An internal state in which a vowel is treated as stressed for reduction purposes but gets no stress mark in the output, for example in monosyllables: дом /dom/.
Secondary Stress
A weaker stress marked ˌ, entered with a grave accent (U+0300), e.g. воѐнно-морско́й /vɐˌjenːə mɐrˈskoj/.
Hard Consonant
A consonant with no palatal articulation (твёрдый), e.g. the n in кон /kon/.
Soft (Palatalized) Consonant
A consonant pronounced with the middle of the tongue raised toward the hard palate, marked ʲ, e.g. конь /konʲ/.
Iotated Vowel
The letters е, ё, ю, я, which stand for /j/ + vowel at the start of a word and after another vowel or a sign, but only soften the preceding consonant after a consonant: я́ма /ˈjamə/ vs. мя́со /ˈmʲasə/.
Soft Sign (ь) / Hard Sign (ъ)
Letters that have no sound of their own: ь softens the preceding consonant, ъ keeps it hard and separates it from an iotated vowel.
Hushing Consonants
The sounds spelled ж, ш (always hard) and ч, щ (always soft), plus ц (hard except before я, ю, ё, ь; an affricate).
Retroflex (ʂ, ʐ)
The symbols the module uses for hard ш ʂ and hard ж ʐ.
Alveolo-palatal (ɕ, ʑ, t͡ɕ)
The soft hushing sounds: щ is the long ɕː, ч is the affricate t͡ɕ.
Affricate
A complex consonant beginning as a stop and releasing as a fricative, written with a tie bar: t͡s (ц), t͡ɕ (ч), d͡ʐ (дж).
Final Devoicing
Voiced obstruents become voiceless at the end of a word: хлеб /xlʲep/, год /ɡot/.
Voicing Assimilation
An obstruent takes over the voicing of the following obstruent: во́дка /ˈvotkə/ (д → [t]), сбор /zbor/ (с → [z]).
Gemination
A long (doubled) consonant, marked ː, e.g. ва́нна /ˈvanːə/. In most positions the module removes orthographic doubling, e.g. кла́сс /ˈkɫas/.
Clitic / Liaison Tie
The tie ‿ joins an unstressed preposition or particle to the next or previous word as one phonetic word: под сто́лом /pɐt͡s‿ˈstoɫəm/.
Foot Boundary
The symbol |, which the module inserts for a comma or dash: /ˈdomə | ɐ‿nʲɪ‿f‿ˈʂkolʲe/.
Optional Segment
A segment in parentheses can be pronounced or omitted: (j) in ябло́ко /(j)ɪˈbɫokə/, (ː) in два́дцать /ˈdvat͡s(ː)ɨtʲ/, and ⁽ʲ⁾ (optional palatalization) in вперёд /f⁽ʲ⁾pʲɪˈrʲɵt/.
Fronting
The shift of /a/ to æ and of /u/ to ʉ between soft consonants: пять /pʲætʲ/, лю́ди /ˈlʲʉdʲɪ/.
Retraction
The shift of /e/ to ɛ and of /i/ to ɨ after the always-hard ж, ш, ц: цена́ /t͡sɨˈna/, ши́на /ˈʂɨnə/.
Schwa
The neutral vowel ə, found in most unstressed а/о syllables not directly before the stress.
Lexicon (V4)
The compressed word list used by the app. Instead of storing IPA, the V4 format stores the stressed-vowel position of each word form, and the module generates the IPA from it.
Homograph
Words spelled alike but stressed differently, e.g. за́мок (castle) and замо́к (lock). Each form is transcribed separately and the results can be cycled by clicking.
Hiatus
Two adjacent vowels in different syllables; in the module two adjacent reduced vowels merge to ɐ‿ɐ, e.g. по абази́ну /pɐ‿ɐbɐˈzʲinʊ/.
NFD
Unicode Normalization Form Decomposed: accents are split off as combining characters (the module reads the stress as U+0301 after the vowel).

Interactive Features

  • Click words to cycle variants: Words not found in the lexicon show one candidate for every possible stress position. Click any transcribed word to cycle through the alternatives.
  • Audio playback: Click the speaker icon next to any word or line to hear text-to-speech pronunciation (requires browser TTS support).
  • Export results: Use PDF or CSV buttons to save transcriptions in your preferred format.
  • Force the stress: Type a combining acute accent (U+0301) after the vowel you want stressed. Typing ё already implies the stress.

Multiple Pronunciation Variants

For Russian, a word can have several candidate transcriptions for different reasons:

  • Homographs with different stress: Lexicon entries such as за́мок / замо́к contain several stressed forms; each is transcribed and shown as a separate variant.
  • One variant per stress position: For words missing from the lexicon the app generates a transcription per vowel, for example /ˈmuɕːɪnə/,/mʊˈɕːinə/,/mʊɕːɪˈna/. Only one of them is usually correct.
  • Module-internal alternatives: In a few clusters the module itself returns two pronunciations: the non-palatal and palatal variants of зж/жж (дрожжи́ /drɐˈʐːɨ/, /drɐˈʑːi/), or soft and hard realizations of a vowel before an optionally soft consonant (ля́мпя /ˈlʲampʲə/, /ˈlʲæmʲpʲə/).
  • Lexicon vs. generated: Dictionary lookup and rule-based generation can differ only in the choice of stress; the sound rules are the same.

When multiple variants exist, click the word to cycle through them. The currently selected variant will be used for PDF/CSV export.

Russian Pronunciation Guide

This guide explains the pronunciation rules that the Wiktionary module actually implements. Russian spelling is only partly phonetic: vowels change with stress, consonants soften before certain vowels, and neighboring consonants influence each other's voicing and softness. The sections below follow the processing order of the module wherever possible; the Implementation Details section lists the exact stages.

Note: the app splits the input at spaces and transcribes each word on its own, and it strips punctuation. The liaison tie ‿, the foot boundary | and all cross-word effects below therefore show the capability of the module (which accepts whole phrases) and will not appear in the app's output.

Stress and Vowel Reduction (Akanye & Ikanye)

The module's allophones table gives each vowel three values: the stressed value, the value in the pretonic syllable (immediately before a primary or secondary stress), and the value elsewhere (all other unstressed syllables). The pretonic slot is only used before a real stress mark (acute or grave), not before a tertiary stress.

Word-Initial Unstressed а and о

An unstressed а or о at the very beginning of a word is always ɐ, even when it is not in the pretonic syllable: океа́н /ɐkʲɪˈan/, авто́бус /ɐfˈtobʊs/.

Unstressed э

A word-initial unstressed э, and an unstressed э directly after a vowel, are rewritten as и before the vowel table is applied: эта́ж /ɪˈtaʂ/, аэропо́рт /ɐɪrɐˈport/. After a consonant it is reduced to ɨ (рэкети́р /rɨkʲɪˈtʲir/). Word-final unstressed -э is kept unreduced.

Adjacent Reduced Vowels

When a schwa is followed by another [ɐ] or [ə] (also across a joined word boundary), both become ɐ: по абази́ну /pɐ‿ɐbɐˈzʲinʊ/.

Optional [j] Before ɪ

A /j/ before an unstressed ɪ is optional when it stands at the start of a word or after a vowel: ябло́ко /(j)ɪˈbɫokə/, по-мо́ему /pɐ‿ˈmo(j)ɪmʊ/, ещё /(j)ɪˈɕːɵ/.

Stress Marks, Monosyllables and ё

  • Acute accent (U+0301): primary stress, shown as ˈ before the stressed syllable: кни́га /ˈknʲiɡə/.
  • Grave accent (U+0300): secondary stress, shown as ˌ: воѐнно-морско́й /vɐˌjenːə mɐrˈskoj/.
  • Circumflex (U+0302): "tertiary" stress. The vowel is treated as stressed for the purposes of reduction, but no stress mark is printed. The module adds it by itself to single-syllable words (see below).
  • Dot above (U+0307) and dot below (U+0323): the dot above prevents the module from adding any stress; the dot below on я or а asks for the semi-reduced value [ɐ]/[ə] instead of [ɪ].

In the app only combining marks survive input cleaning; the ASCII shorthands `, @ and ^ that the module accepts on Wiktionary are removed before the text reaches it.

Monosyllables

A word with a single vowel that carries no accent, is not an unstressed particle or preposition, and is not a prefix or suffix, receives tertiary stress: its vowel is not reduced but no mark is shown: дом /dom/, ель /jelʲ/, сын /sɨn/. If such a word stands next to an unstressed preposition or particle, it receives a real primary stress instead: на дом /nɐ‿ˈdom/.

ё

The letter ё is always a stressed vowel. If a word contains no explicit primary stress and has more than one vowel, the module adds an acute accent to its last ё: самолёт /səmɐˈlʲɵt/. A monosyllabic word with ё gets no printed mark: лёд /lʲɵt/, всё /fsʲɵ/. If the user marks another vowel as stressed, the ё loses its stress, which is why the fallback variants of ё-words can look odd (see Common Issues).

Hard and Soft Consonants (Palatalization)

Most Russian consonants come in hard and soft pairs. Softness is marked by ʲ. The module softens a consonant when it is followed by е, и, ё, ю, я or the soft sign ь, as implemented by the substitution of ʲ after any of m n p b t d k g f v s z x ɣ r l that is followed by a front or iotated vowel or by ь.

Always Hard and Always Soft

  • Always hard: ж, ш, even before и, е, ю, я (see Hushing Consonants), and ц, which is softened only before я, ю, ё and ь: Цю́рих /ˈt͡sʲʉrʲɪx/.
  • Always soft: ч, щ and й (the module adds ʲ to them internally and removes it again, so you never see it): чай /t͡ɕæj/, щёки /ˈɕːɵkʲɪ/.

Assimilative Softening in Clusters

A consonant also softens when a soft consonant follows it. The module has three groups of rules:

  • Always: н before т, д (ба́нтик /ˈbanʲtʲɪk/), before ч, щ (кончи́ть /kɐnʲˈt͡ɕitʲ/); х before к (мя́гкий /ˈmʲæxʲkʲɪj/); the pairs т/д before н and с/з before т/д when no р or л precedes (пя́тница /ˈpʲætʲnʲɪt͡sə/, жи́знь /ˈʐɨzʲnʲ/, сте́на /ˈsʲtʲenə/, здесь /zʲdʲesʲ/, сде́лать /ˈzʲdʲeɫətʲ/).
  • Optionally (shown as ⁽ʲ⁾): сл, зл (бессмы́сленно /bʲɪsːˈmɨs⁽ʲ⁾lʲɪn(ː)ə/, безразли́чно /bʲɪzrɐz⁽ʲ⁾ˈlʲit͡ɕnə/), нс, нз, мп, мб, мф, фм; вп, вб at the start of a word (вперёд /f⁽ʲ⁾pʲɪˈrʲɵt/); labials before /j/ (семья́ /sʲɪˈm⁽ʲ⁾ja/); final -лся (учи́лся /ʊˈt͡ɕiɫs⁽ʲ⁾ə/); labial + ь before ст- (ста́вьте /ˈstaf⁽ʲ⁾tʲe/).
  • Never: in most other clusters a hard consonant stays hard before a soft one, e.g. the t in тря́пка /ˈtrʲapkə/.

The Soft Sign ь and Hard Sign ъ

  • ь softens the preceding consonant and has no sound itself: соль /solʲ/, тетра́дь /tʲɪˈtratʲ/. Before и it adds a /j/ (семьи́ /sʲɪˈm⁽ʲ⁾ji/), and ьо is read like ьё: Асунсьо́н /ɐsʊn⁽ʲ⁾ˈsʲjɵn/.
  • ъ keeps the preceding consonant hard and makes the following iotated vowel keep its /j/: объе́ктом /ɐbˈjektəm/, подъе́зд /pɐdˈjest/. A hard sign before a non-iotated vowel (rare) is turned into a glottal stop ʔ. A word-final ъ in pre-reform spelling is ignored: хлѣ́бъ /ˈxlʲep/.

Hushing Consonants: ж ш ц ч щ

Retraction After ж, ш, ц

These consonants are always hard, so a following и is pronounced ɨ and a following е is pronounced ɛ, regardless of spelling (module table retracting): жи́знь /ˈʐɨzʲnʲ/, ши́на /ˈʂɨnə/, цирк /t͡sɨrk/, цена́ /t͡sɨˈna/, же́ны /ˈʐɛnɨ/, цель /t͡sɛlʲ/.

A written ю or ё after ж, ш is pronounced without the /j/ and without softening: жюри́ /ʐʊˈrʲi/, брошю́ра /brɐˈʂurə/, жёлтый /ˈʐoɫtɨj/.

Soft ч and щ

Within a word and across a word boundary, obstruents agree in voicing with the following obstruent. The module repeats the assimilation until nothing changes, so that whole clusters are handled.

  • тс, дз become the short affricates t͡s, d͡z: де́тство /ˈdʲet͡stvə/. Across words, before a vowel, and after the prefixes от-, под- they become long (t͡s + s): отсу́тствие /ɐt͡sˈsut͡stvʲɪje/, под сто́лом /pɐt͡s‿ˈstoɫəm/, от зо́лота /ɐd͡z‿ˈzoɫətə/.
  • тш, дж, чш give the hard affricates t͡ʂ, d͡ʐ: лу́чший /ˈɫut͡ʂʂɨj/, дже́м /ˈd͡ʐʐɛm/.
  • дц, тц, дч, тч before a vowel are always long: отцо́м /ɐˈt͡sːom/; otherwise the doubling is treated as ordinary gemination and normally disappears: оте́ц /ɐˈtʲet͡s/.
  • -тся, -ться (with the default part of speech) are pronounced as t͡s plus schwa, and the affricate is long when the stressed vowel directly precedes the т: у́чится /ˈut͡ɕɪt͡sə/, учи́ться /ʊˈt͡ɕit͡sːə/, мы́ться /ˈmɨt͡sːə/.
  • ч before a voiced obstruent is voiced to d͡ʑ: до́чь бы /ˈdod͡ʑ‿bɨ/.

Doubled Consonants (Gemination)

Doubled letters are first marked as long (ː), and most of the length is removed again in a later step. The default rules are:

  • л is always degeminated.
  • Length is kept between vowels directly after the stressed vowel: ва́нна /ˈvanːə/, гру́ппа /ˈɡrupːə/, програ́мма /prɐˈɡramːə/, дли́нный /ˈdlʲinːɨj/; also ж and н between vowels elsewhere in the word, and сск / зск directly after the stress: ру́сский /ˈrusːkʲɪj/.
  • Optional length (ː) for н between vowels after the stress but not directly after it: бессмы́сленно /bʲɪsːˈmɨs⁽ʲ⁾lʲɪn(ː)ə/.
  • щ and the soft ж (ʑː) are always long.
  • At recognized prefix boundaries (без-/бес-, воз-/вос-, из-/ис-, от-, об-, под-, над-, пред-, раз-/рас-, с-/з-, меж-, транс-, контр-, супер-, also after не-/по-, and others from the geminate_pref table) the doubling is always kept: расска́з /rɐsːˈkas/, о́тдых /ˈodːɨx/, ссо́ра /ˈsːorə/, бессмы́сленно /bʲɪsːˈmɨs⁽ʲ⁾lʲɪn(ː)ə/.
  • Everywhere else doubling disappears: кла́сс /ˈkɫas/.

Spelling Exceptions: -ого, что, мягк-

The transliteration helper apply_tr_fixes rewrites the Cyrillic spelling of a few frequent patterns before any sound rule runs:

  • -ого, -его → -ово, -ево: the г of adjective and pronoun genitive endings is pronounced [v]: кра́сного /ˈkrasnəvə/, большо́го /bɐlʲˈʂovə/, его́ /(j)ɪˈvo/, кого́ /kɐˈvo/, чего́ /t͡ɕɪˈvo/, того́ /tɐˈvo/. Likewise сегодня: сего́дня /sʲɪˈvodʲnʲə/.
  • Exceptions where the г is kept (an explicit list of spellings, including много, дорого, строго, убого, ого, лого, Диего): мно́го /ˈmnoɡə/, до́рого /ˈdorəɡə/, стро́го /ˈstroɡə/, убо́го /ʊˈboɡə/, ого́ /ɐˈɡo/, ло́го /ˈɫoɡə/, Дие́го /dʲɪˈjeɡə/. Because the check is made on the written form, a stress variant that puts the accent on the final -о is not recognized as the exception.
  • что, чтобы, ничто → ш: что́ /ˈʂto/, чтобы /ʂtəbɨ/, ничто́ /nʲɪʂˈto/, что-то /ˈʂto‿tə/.
  • мягк-, мягч-, лёгк-, легч- → -хк-, -хч-: мя́гкий /ˈmʲæxʲkʲɪj/, лёгкий /ˈlʲɵxʲkʲɪj/, ле́гче /ˈlʲext͡ɕe/.

Prepositions, Particles and Hyphens

Note: the app splits the input at spaces and transcribes each word on its own, and it strips punctuation. The liaison tie ‿, the foot boundary | and all cross-word effects below therefore show the capability of the module (which accepts whole phrases) and will not appear in the app's output.

Certain unaccented words are not stressed and are joined to a neighboring word with the tie ‿ so that they reduce and assimilate with it (module table accentless):

  • Join with the following word: без, близ, в, во, да, до, за, из, из-под, из-за, изо, к, ко, меж, на, над, надо, не, ни, об, обо, от, ото, перед, передо, по, под, подо, пред, предо, при, про, с, со, у, через; and а, о only when a space (not a hyphen) follows.
  • Join with the preceding word: бы, б, ж, же, ли, либо, ль, ка, нибудь, тка; то only after a hyphen.

Examples: не зна́ю /nʲɪ‿ˈznajʊ/, на столе́ /nə‿stɐˈlʲe/, ты́ же /ˈtɨ‿ʐɨ/, говори́л ли /ɡəvɐˈrʲiɫ‿lʲɪ/, что-то /ˈʂto‿tə/, а ведь /ɐ‿ˈvʲetʲ/. The same word written with a stress mark is not a clitic, which changes the whole phrase: по не́бу /pɐ‿ˈnʲebʊ/ vs. по́ небу /ˈpo nʲɪbʊ/, а ведь /ɐ‿ˈvʲetʲ/ vs. а́ вот /ˈa vot/.

The words не and же are special-cased so that their final -е is not treated as an ordinary word-final -е: they become nʲɪ and ʐɨ. After a joined consonant, и turns into ɨ: под игло́й /pəd‿ɨˈɡɫoj/. A hyphen inside a word is converted into a space (кое-кто́ /kəjə ˈkto/), and a leading or trailing hyphen marks a prefix or suffix, which is never stressed.

Fronting of а and у Between Soft Consonants

When a or u (including ʊ) stands between two soft consonants, it is fronted to æ or ʉ: пять /pʲætʲ/, ня́ня /ˈnʲænʲə/, лю́ди /ˈlʲʉdʲɪ/, чай /t͡ɕæj/, ща́вель /ˈɕːævʲɪlʲ/. Next to a hard consonant nothing changes: мя́со /ˈmʲasə/.

If the following consonant is only optionally soft, the module returns two pronunciations, one with a hard consonant and an unfronted vowel, and one with a soft consonant and a fronted vowel: ля́мпя /ˈlʲampʲə/, /ˈlʲæmʲpʲə/, тя́нзи /ˈtʲanzʲɪ/, /ˈtʲænʲzʲɪ/, сча́стливым /ˈɕːaslʲɪvɨm/, /ˈɕːæsʲlʲɪvɨm/.

Hard л (Dark L)

A hard л is written as the dark ɫ, a soft л as lʲ: лук /ɫuk/ vs. люк /lʲuk/, ма́сло /ˈmasɫə/, жёлтый /ˈʐoɫtɨj/.

Punctuation and Special Input

  • Commas and dashes inside the text become the foot boundary |: /ˈdomə | ɐ‿nʲɪ‿f‿ˈʂkolʲe/.
  • ! and ? are treated as separate words so that they do not disturb the word-final rules: /da! nʲet?/.
  • Apostrophes in foreign names are dropped.
  • Pre-reform and Old Russian letters (ѣ, і, ѳ, ѵ, ъ at the end of a word and others) are converted to their modern equivalents: хлѣ́бъ /ˈxlʲep/.
  • Capital letters are lowercased before processing; digits are dropped by the app.

Using the Tool: Practical Notes

Why does one word sometimes show several transcriptions, and how do I choose?

Spelling does not show stress, so a word can map to more than one pronunciation. Homographs with different stress, such as за́мок /ˈzamək/ and замо́к /zɐˈmok/, are listed as separate variants. A word that is not in the lexicon is transcribed once for every possible stress position. Click the word to cycle through the variants and keep the one that matches the word you mean; see Multiple Pronunciation Variants. Open in the converter

How do I mark the stress myself?

Type a combining acute accent (U+0301) directly after the stressed vowel, for example рука́ /rʊˈka/. The letter ё counts as stressed when no other stress mark is present, and a combining grave accent (U+0300) gives secondary stress. A word typed with a stress mark, or containing ё, is transcribed exactly as marked and gives a single result. Details are in Stress Marks, Monosyllables and ё.

Why can the output differ from a pronunciation dictionary?

The module works from spelling and stress only. It does not know lexical exceptions, for example the vowel of the loanword кафе́ /kɐˈfʲe/, which speakers pronounce with [ɛ] after a hard consonant, and it does not implement the dropped в in -вств- or -чн- as [ʂn]. Dialects and fast-speech reductions are not modeled. See Dialects & Limitations and Common Issues.

Is the transcription phonemic or phonetic?

Phonetic. The Wiktionary module produces only a narrow phonetic transcription for Russian, so the app offers no phonemic form; see Phonemic vs Phonetic.

Implementation Details (for Developers)

Russian transcription is done in two layers: a JavaScript handler in scripts/utils.js (ipaHandlers.Russian) that finds out the stress, and the Lua module lua_modules/ru-pron_wasm.lua (the Wiktionary ru-pron module, Benwing2's version), which turns one stress-marked phrase into IPA. Two helpers are required: ru-translit.lua (apply_tr_fixes, tr_after_fixes) and ru-common.lua (decompose, remove_grave_accents and character classes). The Lua module is called as ru_ipa.ipa_string(text), which returns the list of pronunciations produced by export.ipa joined with ", ".

22. Brackets and Fronting of a and u

If bracket is set the words are enclosed in [ ] (the app does not use it). Then, to make the sequence rules easy, ʲ is added temporarily to the inherently soft č ǰ ɕ ӂ j. a, u and ʊ between two soft consonants (ʲ + vowel + any consonant + ʲ) are replaced by æ and ʉ using the table fronting; a vowel between a soft consonant and an optionally soft one produces two output strings.

24. Result

ru_ipa_main returns a list (one entry per variant). export.ipa concatenates the lists of all spelling variants, and export.ipa_string joins them with ", ". This is why the ж-variants appear inside one pair of slashes in the app when the handler's fallback path is used (see Known Issues).

Other Limitations

  • By design the module renders unstressed final -е as [e] after a soft consonant (по́ле /ˈpolʲe/); other realizations (/ˈpolʲə/, /ˈpolʲɪ/) need the pos option, which the app does not pass.
  • Stress is only known from the lexicon (or typed by the user); the module never guesses it.
  • The rules cover standard Moscow pronunciation. Dialects and fast-speech reductions are not modeled.
  • Numbers, symbols and Latin letters inside Russian words are dropped or passed through unchanged.
  • The module's own FIXME list mentions open issues such as the pronunciation of final unstressed -е before a pause and -э after paired consonants in loanwords.
