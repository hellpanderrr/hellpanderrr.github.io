About This Tool

This Ukrainian transcription app uses the Wiktionary Ukrainian Pronunciation Module to generate phonetic transcriptions for Ukrainian text. IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) is a standardized system for transcribing speech sounds. This tool converts Ukrainian Cyrillic spelling into IPA, helping learners, linguists, and developers understand pronunciation.

Ukrainian spelling is close to phonemic, but the stress is not written in ordinary text, and stress decides how vowels sound (for example, unstressed а becomes /ɐ/). The transcription therefore needs to know which vowel is stressed. The app uses a comprehensive lexicon extracted from Wiktionary data dumps (a "V4" lexicon that stores the position of the stressed vowel for each word form). For words found in the lexicon, the browser chooses the stress automatically. For words that are not found, the app generates one transcription for every possible stress position, and you click the word to cycle through them.

The examples on this page show the rule path: each one is the output of the real pronunciation module called on a word with its stress marked (for example вода́). In the app, a lexicon word reaches the same module with the stress taken from the dictionary, so the result is the same transcription.

Dialects & Limitations

  • Supported: Standard Ukrainian (literary) pronunciation, style "Default".
  • Not supported: Regional dialects (Carpathian, Polissian, etc.).
  • Edge cases: Proper names, recent loanwords, and words missing from the lexicon need the right stress variant to be picked by you. See Common Issues & Limitations.
  • Word-by-word: Each word is transcribed independently. Cross-word effects (such as the в / у and і / й alternation after vowels, or sandhi between words) are not modeled.
  • Input script: Text must be Cyrillic. A word that mixes in Latin look-alike letters is returned unchanged instead of transcribed.

Phonemic vs Phonetic

  • Phonetic (the only form available for Ukrainian): A detailed, narrow transcription that shows non-distinctive variations in pronunciation (allophones), for example unstressed vowel reduction (/ɐ/, /ʊ/, /e/), softened consonants (/lʲ/), and the several sounds of the letter в.
  • Phonemic: Not offered. The Wiktionary module produces only a phonetic transcription.

Quick Reference (Glossary)

Phoneme
The smallest unit of sound in a language that distinguishes meaning (e.g., /p/ vs. /b/ in English "pat" vs. "bat").
Allophone
A variant pronunciation of a phoneme that doesn't change meaning, e.g. в as /ʋ/ in ві́тер /ˈʋʲiter/ but as /w/ in вода́ /wɔˈda/.
Stress (Наголос)
The syllable pronounced with most force. In Ukrainian it is free (can fall on any syllable) and is marked in dictionaries with an acute accent, e.g. вода́. The IPA mark ˈ goes before the stressed syllable.
Acute / Grave Accent
Combining marks U+0301 (acute) and U+0300 (grave). The module turns an acute into primary stress ˈ and a grave into secondary stress ˌ, e.g. ра̀діоприйма́ч /ˌradʲiɔprei̯ˈmat͡ʃ/.
Palatalization (Softening)
A consonant pronounced with the tongue raised toward the hard palate, written ʲ. Triggered by ь and by the vowels і, є, ю, я, e.g. сі́ль /ˈsʲilʲ/.
Soft Sign (М'який знак, ь)
A letter with no sound of its own that marks the preceding consonant as soft, e.g. кінь.
Apostrophe (Апостроф)
The letter ' written before я, ю, є, ї to keep the preceding consonant hard and pronounce a separate /j/, e.g. м'я́со /ˈmjasɔ/.
Iotated Vowel
The letters є, ї, ю, я, which spell a vowel preceded by /j/ (word-initially, after a vowel, or after an apostrophe) or a softened consonant plus vowel (after a consonant).
Vowel Reduction
Change of an unstressed vowel to a weaker quality. In this module: a→ɐ, u→ʊ, ɛ, ɪ→e, and ɔ→o before a stressed u or i.
Voicing Assimilation
A voiceless obstruent becomes voiced before a voiced obstruent, e.g. вокза́л /wɔɡˈzaɫ/ (k → ɡ before z). Ukrainian has no devoicing of voiced consonants at the end of a word.
Final Devoicing
The process by which voiced consonants become voiceless at word ends (German, Russian). It does not occur in this Ukrainian transcription: зуб /zʊb/ keeps b.
Akanye
Russian-type merging of unstressed о and а. Not implemented here: unstressed о stays /ɔ/ (сторона́ /stɔrɔˈna/).
Affricate
A stop released as a fricative at the same place, written with a tie bar: /t͡s/ (ц), /t͡ʃ/ (ч), /d͡z/ (дз), /d͡ʒ/ (дж).
Tie Bar
The combining mark ͡ joining two letters into one affricate.
Hushing Consonants
The "sh-type" sounds ж, ч, ш, щ (ʒ, t͡ʃ, ʃ, ʃt͡ʃ).
Hissing Consonants
The "s-type" sounds з, с, ц, дз (z, s, t͡s, d͡z).
Geminate (Long) Consonant
A consonant held longer, written ː, e.g. ві́лла /ˈʋʲiɫːɐ/.
Glide (Non-syllabic Vowel)
A vowel-like sound that does not form a syllable, marked ̯, e.g. /i̯/ and /u̯/ in ма́йже /ˈmai̯ʒe/ and авто́ /ɐu̯ˈtɔ/.
Dark L
The velarized /ɫ/ used for non-soft л, e.g. по́ле /ˈpɔɫe/.
Lexicon (V4)
A dictionary generated from Wiktionary dumps. For Ukrainian it stores, for each word form, a stressed spelling (the V4 format records the position of the stressed vowel). One word can have several stressed forms, e.g. "замо́к,за́мок".
Foot Boundary
The symbol |. In the module, commas and dashes inside the text become |. The app removes punctuation before calling the module, so you normally never see it.
Word Boundary Marker
The module internally wraps every word in #…# so rules can refer to word beginnings and ends. It is stripped before output.

Interactive Features

  • Click words to cycle variants: When a word is not in the lexicon, every possible stress position is generated. Click the transcribed word to cycle through them until you reach the correct one.
  • Force the stress yourself: You can type a combining acute accent (U+0301) after a vowel to mark the stress. See the known issue about how the app currently handles a mark you type.
  • Audio playback: Click the speaker icon next to any word or line to hear text-to-speech pronunciation (requires browser TTS support).
  • Export results: Use PDF or CSV buttons to save transcriptions in your preferred format.

Multiple Pronunciation Variants

For Ukrainian, a word can show several transcriptions:

  • Different stress positions: Words not found in the lexicon get one transcription per vowel. For мама the app produces /ˈmamɐ/ and /mɐˈma/.
  • Homographs with different stress: Dictionary entries such as "замо́к,за́мок" give one transcription per stressed form, joined as /ipa1/,/ipa2/. For example замо́к (lock) is /zɐˈmɔk/ and за́мок (castle) is /ˈzamɔk/.
  • Impossible variants are included: Since any vowel may be stressed, some variants are not real Ukrainian pronunciations. Pick the one that matches the word you mean.

When multiple variants exist, click the word to cycle through them. The currently selected variant will be used for PDF/CSV export.

Ukrainian Pronunciation Guide

This guide explains how the module turns Ukrainian spelling into sounds. Ukrainian orthography is largely phonemic: most letters have one sound. The complications are free stress, palatalization (softening), and a few assimilations. Every example below is real engine output for a word with its stress marked.

Stress (Наголос)

Ukrainian stress is free and unmarked in ordinary writing, so the module needs it as input.

  • Marked with an acute accent: The accent sits on the stressed vowel (вода́, кни́га). The module converts it to ˈ and places it at the start of the syllable: вода́ /wɔˈda/, кни́га /ˈknɪɦɐ/.
  • Stress goes before the onset cluster: The mark is moved back over the consonants that begin the syllable: сестра́ /seˈstra/, кни́жка /ˈknɪʒkɐ/, Дніпро́ /dʲnʲiˈprɔ/.
  • Between two consonants: When the syllable boundary falls inside a cluster, the mark stands before the last consonant (or between two affricates): відчу́ти /ʋʲid͡ʒˈt͡ʃute/. Clusters that can begin a word stay together: захисти́ти /zɐxeˈstɪte/.
  • Before iotated vowels: The mark sits before the /j/: моє́ /mɔˈjɛ/, Украї́на /ʊkrɐˈjinɐ/.
  • Secondary stress: A grave accent in the input gives ˌ: ра̀діоприйма́ч /ˌradʲiɔprei̯ˈmat͡ʃ/.
  • Monosyllables in the app: A one-vowel word is given a stressed vowel before transcription, so не appears as /ˈnɛ/ (a full vowel), although in connected speech such small words are usually unstressed. See Common Issues.
Note: For words found in the lexicon, you do not need to type any accent: the app takes the stress from the dictionary. For other words, use the click-to-cycle feature to choose the right variant.

Unstressed Vowels

Unstressed vowels change as follows (stress mark is the only trigger; there is no reduction of і):

  • а → ɐ: дава́ти /dɐˈʋate/ (stressed: ма́ма /ˈmamɐ/, where the final unstressed а is /ɐ/).
  • у → ʊ: суша́ /sʊˈʃa/, узу́м /ʊˈzum/.
  • е, и → e: unstressed е and и sound the same: лимо́н /ɫeˈmɔn/, кури́ти /kʊˈrɪte/ (unstressed и in the last syllable is /e/).
  • о → ɔ (kept): сторона́ /stɔrɔˈna/. There is no "akanye" (о does not become а).
  • о → o before stressed у/і: the vowel is assimilated and closed: коні́ /koˈnʲi/, ходжу́ /xoˈd͡ʒu/, подру́га /poˈdruɦɐ/.
  • і is never reduced: кіно́ /kʲiˈnɔ/.

Iotated Vowels (є, ї, ю, я) and the Apostrophe

  • є = /jɛ/, ї = /ji/, ю = /ju/, я = /ja/ at the start of a word or after a vowel: ї́жа /ˈjiʒɐ/, моє́ /mɔˈjɛ/, юна́к /jʊˈnak/, я́блуко /ˈjabɫʊkɔ/.
  • After a consonant the /j/ is absorbed and the consonant becomes soft: лю́ди /ˈlʲude/, тя́га /ˈtʲaɦɐ/, мо́ря /ˈmɔrʲɐ/.
  • After the apostrophe the consonant stays hard and /j/ is pronounced: м'я́со /ˈmjasɔ/, об'є́кт /ɔˈbjɛkt/, комп'ю́тер /kɔmˈpjuter/, сім'я́ /sʲiˈmja/.
  • Typographic apostrophe: The curved ’ (U+2019) is converted to the straight ' by the app before transcription, so both spellings give the same result in the app.

Soft Consonants and the Soft Sign (ь)

  • Before і: Almost every consonant is softened before the vowel і: лі́то /ˈlʲitɔ/, кі́т /ˈkʲit/, гі́рка /ˈɦʲirkɐ/, ві́тер /ˈʋʲiter/, ці́лий /ˈt͡sʲiɫei̯/, чі́тко /ˈt͡ʃʲitkɔ/.
  • Hushing consonants before і: ж, ш (and ч) are also marked soft before і by this module: ші́сть /ˈʃʲisʲtʲ/, жі́нка /ˈʒʲinkɐ/ (Ukrainian orthoepy describes these as slightly softened).
  • Soft sign: ь gives ʲ to the preceding consonant: сі́ль /ˈsʲilʲ/, до́ля /ˈdɔlʲɐ/, ма́ть /ˈmatʲ/.
  • Softness spreads backward: In a cluster of two consonants where the second is soft, a preceding т, д, с, з, н, л is also soft: пі́сня /ˈpʲisʲnʲɐ/, ра́дість /ˈradʲisʲtʲ/, ма́ленький /ˈmaɫenʲkei̯/.
  • Labials do not soften their neighbor: лі́пня /ˈlʲipnʲɐ/ keeps a hard p.
  • Not softened: a consonant before the apostrophe (see above).

Voicing Assimilation (and Why There Is No Final Devoicing)

A voiceless obstruent becomes voiced when a voiced obstruent (b d z ʒ ɡ ɦ) follows it:

  • вокза́л /wɔɡˈzaɫ/ (к → ɡ), футбо́л /fʊdˈbɔɫ/ (т → d), про́сьба /ˈprɔzʲbɐ/ (сь → zʲ), боротьба́ /bɔrɔdʲˈba/ (тьб → dʲb), про́шба /ˈprɔʒbɐ/ (ш → ʒ), ру́кзак /ˈruɡzɐk/.
  • One direction only: A voiced consonant is not devoiced before a voiceless one: ві́дпустка /ˈʋʲidpʊstkɐ/, кі́гті /ˈkʲiɦtʲi/, ле́гко /ˈɫɛɦkɔ/.
  • No final devoicing: Voiced consonants stay voiced at the end of a word: ніж /nʲiʒ/, рід /rʲid/, ніг /nʲiɦ/, зуб /zʊb/, дуб /dʊb/. (These monosyllables were passed to the module without stress; in the app they are stressed, which changes only the vowel.)
  • В and sonorants do not trigger voicing: в and sonorants (m, n, l, r) are not in the trigger set, so тві́р /ˈtʲʋʲir/ keeps a voiceless t.

Г and Ґ

  • г = /ɦ/ (voiced glottal fricative, the usual sound): га́нок /ˈɦanɔk/, ци́ган /ˈt͡sɪɦɐn/, ге́нерал /ˈɦɛnerɐɫ/.
  • ґ = /ɡ/ (stop; rare, mainly in loanwords and a few native words): ґа́нок /ˈɡanɔk/, ґо́лос /ˈɡɔɫɔs/.
  • Voicing interacts with these letters: before a voiced obstruent, к becomes /ɡ/ (ру́кзак /ˈruɡzɐk/) and х becomes /ɦ/. The letter г itself is never devoiced.

The Letter В: [ʋ], [w], [u̯], [ʍ]

The letter в is one phoneme with four realizations. The module applies them in this order:

  • [u̯] after a vowel and before a consonant or the end of the word (syllable coda): ле́в /ˈɫɛu̯/, ко́ров /ˈkɔrɔu̯/, пра́вда /ˈprau̯dɐ/, авто́ /ɐu̯ˈtɔ/, ді́вчина /ˈdʲiu̯t͡ʃenɐ/.
  • [w] before /ɔ, u/ and before voiced consonants (when not already [u̯]): вода́ /wɔˈda/, вуха́ /wʊˈxa/, про́вулок /ˈprɔwʊɫɔk/, лі́гво /ˈlʲiɦwɔ/.
  • [ʍ] before a voiceless consonant: вкла́д /ˈʍkɫad/, вчо́ра /ˈʍt͡ʃɔrɐ/, вхі́д /ˈʍxʲid/.
  • [ʋ] everywhere else (before other vowels, before soft vowels, between vowels): кра́ва /ˈkraʋɐ/, ві́тер /ˈʋʲiter/, шви́дко /ˈʃʋɪdkɔ/, хви́ля /ˈxʋɪlʲɐ/.

A one-letter word в is transcribed as /ʋ/ because words are processed one at a time; in running speech it would behave like [u̯]/[w] after a vowel.

The Letter Й: [j] and [i̯]

  • [j] before a vowel: йо́го /ˈjɔɦɔ/.
  • [i̯] after a vowel and before a consonant or at the end of the word: ма́йже /ˈmai̯ʒe/, мі́й /ˈmʲii̯/, ві́йна /ˈʋʲii̯nɐ/, ба́йка /ˈbai̯kɐ/.
  • [i̯] at the start of a word before a consonant: йти́ /ˈi̯tɪ/.
  • Endings -ий / -ій: The unstressed и reduces to /e/ and the й is a glide: зеле́ний /zeˈɫɛnei̯/, чо́рний /ˈt͡ʃɔrnei̯/.

Affricates and Digraphs (дж, дз, ц, ч, щ)

  • дж = /d͡ʒ/, дз = /d͡z/: дже́рело /ˈd͡ʒɛreɫɔ/, дзе́ркало /ˈd͡zɛrkɐɫɔ/, піджа́к /pʲiˈd͡ʒak/. The sequence is read as an affricate even across a prefix boundary.
  • ц = /t͡s/, ч = /t͡ʃ/: ци́рк /ˈt͡sɪrk/, чо́му /ˈt͡ʃɔmʊ/.
  • щ = /ʃt͡ʃ/ (a sequence, not one sound): ща́стя /ˈʃt͡ʃasʲtʲɐ/, ще́дрий /ˈʃt͡ʃɛdrei̯/.
  • Soft affricates: ґе́дзь /ˈɡɛd͡zʲ/, дзьо́б /ˈd͡zʲɔb/.
  • Dental + sibilant sequences: д or т before a hissing/hushing consonant merges into an affricate: відсо́тки /ʋʲid͡zˈsɔtke/, підши́пник /pʲid͡ʒˈʃɪpnek/, відчу́ти /ʋʲid͡ʒˈt͡ʃute/, ві́дцурати /ˈʋʲid͡zt͡sʊrɐte/, бра́тство /ˈbrat͡stwɔ/, ма́тч /ˈmat͡ʃː/.

Long (Geminate) Consonants

  • Doubled letters are read as one long consonant: ві́лла /ˈʋʲiɫːɐ/, ванна́ /ʋɐˈnːa/, ма́ссою /ˈmasːɔjʊ/, суббо́та /sʊˈbːɔtɐ/, ввечері́ /ʋːet͡ʃeˈrʲi/.
  • Before я, ю, є, ї, і, ь the long consonant is also soft: зна́ння /ˈznanʲːɐ/, ху́ддя /ˈxudʲːɐ/, оббі́гти /ɔˈbʲːiɦte/, ста́ття /ˈstatʲːɐ/.
  • Reflexive endings -ться, -шся: ви́читься /ˈʋɪt͡ʃet͡sʲːɐ/, ви́чишся /ˈʋɪt͡ʃesʲːɐ/.
  • Unwritten length: зжа́ти /ˈʒːate/, сши́ти /ˈʃːɪte/, дочці́ /dɔˈt͡sʲːi/.

Consonant Cluster Changes

The module applies a few spelling-to-pronunciation simplifications that Ukrainian orthography does not show:

  • нтськ → н'ськ: студе́нтський /stʊˈdɛnʲsʲkei̯/.
  • нтст → нст: студе́нтство /stʊˈdɛnstwɔ/.
  • стд → зд: шістдеся́т /ʃʲizdeˈsʲat/.
  • стс → сс: шістсо́т /ʃʲiˈsːɔt/.
  • стч → шч: пі́стчик /ˈpʲiʃt͡ʃek/.
  • зш → жш, зч → жч: безшу́мний /beʒˈʃumnei̯/, розча́рувати /rɔʒˈt͡ʃarʊʋɐte/.
  • Hushing + hissing: ножці́ /nozʲˈt͡sʲi/, дошці́ /doˈsʲt͡sʲi/, дочці́ /dɔˈt͡sʲːi/.
  • Optional length: ві́стці /ˈʋʲisʲt͡sʲ(ː)i/ (length in parentheses is heard only in slow speech).

Dark and Soft L

Using the Tool: Practical Notes

Why does one word sometimes show several transcriptions, and how do I choose?

Words found in the lexicon are transcribed with the stress stored there, and homographs with different stress give one transcription per form: за́мок /ˈzamɔk/ (castle) and замо́к /zɐˈmɔk/ (lock). A word that is not in the lexicon is transcribed once for every possible stress position, and some of those variants are not real pronunciations. Click the word to cycle through them and keep the right one; see Multiple Pronunciation Variants. Open in the converter

Should I type a stress mark myself?

Only if you know the stress. A word typed with an acute accent after the stressed vowel is transcribed exactly as marked and gives a single result: вода́ /wɔˈda/. Without a mark, a word in the lexicon gets its stress from the dictionary, and any other word is offered with one variant per stress position, which you cycle by clicking. See Stress (Наголос) for how the stress mark is placed.

Why can the output differ from a pronunciation dictionary?

Each word is transcribed on its own, so effects across words, such as the в / у and і / й alternation after vowels, are not modeled. A one-vowel word is given a stressed vowel before transcription, so не́ /ˈnɛ/ appears with a full vowel although such words are usually unstressed in speech. Dialects are not supported; see Dialects & Limitations.

Is the transcription phonemic or phonetic?

Phonetic. The Wiktionary module produces only a narrow phonetic transcription for Ukrainian, so no phonemic form is offered; see Phonemic vs Phonetic.

Implementation Details (for Developers)

The transcription is produced by lua_modules/uk-pron_wasm.lua, which is the Wiktionary Ukrainian pronunciation module (function export.pronunciation). It uses helper functions from uk-common.lua (decompose_grave, needs_accents). The module is short: it has no dictionary of its own and is one pass of ordered regular-expression replacements per word. In the tables below the Output column is the final engine output for the whole word, while the Description explains which step produced the visible effect.

Overview of the Pipeline

  1. App handler (scripts/utils.js): sanitize, lexicon lookup, stress variants.
  2. Entry checks: Cyrillic check, lowercase, grave decomposition, stress requirement.
  3. Word split and # wrapping.
  4. Orthographic cluster simplifications and geminates.
  5. Letter mapping (sequences first, single letters last).
  6. Palatalization, voicing, and sibilant assimilation.
  7. Vowel reduction.
  8. Allophones of в and й.
  9. Stress-mark placement and clean-up.

What the Module Does Not Implement

  • No final devoicing and no progressive devoicing (only voiceless → voiced before a voiced obstruent).
  • No akanye: unstressed о stays /ɔ/.
  • No morphology: there is no prefix/suffix analysis and no automatic stress. The helper functions in uk-common.lua for the в/у and і/й alternation (initial_alternation) and for declension are used by other Wiktionary modules, not by this pronunciation function.
  • No cross-word processing: every word is wrapped in # and handled alone.

Known Transcription Problems

This table shows issues found by running the real engine. "System Output" is what the app handler returns in the Node test environment (no lexicon loaded). "Correct IPA" is either the output of the module called directly on a correctly stress-marked word, or a description in words.

не (small word) /ˈnɛ/ /ne/ (unstressed clitic, module on the bare word) Every one-vowel word is stressed by the handler, so function words get full stressed vowels. Also: на gives /ˈna/ instead of /nɐ/; у gives /ˈu/ instead of /ʊ/. Treat the vowel of a short function word as reduced in running speech.
