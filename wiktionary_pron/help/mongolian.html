About This Tool

This Mongolian transcription app uses a modified version of the Wiktionary Mongolian Pronunciation Module to turn Cyrillic Mongolian spelling into a phonemic IPA transcription. IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) is a standardized system for transcribing speech sounds. This tool helps learners, linguists and developers see how a Mongolian word is pronounced. Phonemic transcription represents the abstract sound system (phonemes) rather than every detail of actual speech. The phoneme inventory and the vowel treatment follow The Phonology of Mongolian by Jan-Olof Svantesson et al. (2005), aiming at the Standard Halh dialect as spoken in Ulaanbaatar.

Unlike German, French or Russian in this app, Mongolian uses no dictionary lexicon. Every word is generated by the module's rules alone (the app calls window.mn_ipa.to_IPA(cleanText) directly), so the output is exactly as good as the spelling rules. For example, сайн /saiŋ/ ("good"). Only the Cyrillic script is supported; traditional Mongolian script is not.

Dialects & Limitations

  • Supported: Default = Standard Halh (Khalkha), the official language of Mongolia, written in Cyrillic.
  • Not supported: Inner Mongolian varieties (Chahar and others), Buryat, Oirat dialects, and the traditional vertical script. Unlike Inner Mongolian, Halh keeps diphthongs, and the module restores them (e.g. ай /ai/, not a monophthong).
  • Edge cases: Russian loanwords with е, ё, ю, я after a consonant, words with the letter ы, and words containing ью are handled poorly (see Common Issues).
  • Word-by-word: the app transcribes one space-separated word at a time. Hyphens are deleted before processing, so a hyphenated compound is treated as a single word.

Phonemic vs Phonetic

  • Phonemic (the only form offered): a broad transcription showing only the sounds that distinguish meaning. Mongolian stops contrast aspiration, not voicing, but this module writes the non-aspirated (lenis) series with the voiced symbols b, d, ɡ and the aspirated (fortis) series as pʰ, tʰ, kʰ. For instance бага /baɡ/ uses ɡ, but хот /xɔtʰ/ ends in tʰ.
  • Phonetic: not available for Mongolian.

Quick Reference (Glossary)

Phoneme
The smallest unit of sound that distinguishes meaning. In Mongolian, for example, aspirated /tʰ/ and lenis /d/ are different phonemes: тав /tʰaw̜/ vs. дав /daw̜/.
Allophone
A variant pronunciation of a phoneme that does not change meaning.
Aspiration
A puff of air after a stop, written ʰ. Mongolian п, т, к, ц, ч are aspirated, e.g. цас /t͡sʰas/ "snow".
Lenis / Fortis
The two stop series of Mongolian. Fortis stops (п, т, к, ц, ч) are aspirated; lenis stops (б, д, г, з, ж) are not, and are written here with voiced symbols.
Long Vowel
A vowel written double in Cyrillic (аа, оо, уу, үү, өө, ээ, ий, ии) and transcribed with ː, e.g. хоол /xɔːɮ/ "food".
Diphthong
A vowel plus glide in one syllable, such as ай, ой, ау: сайн /saiŋ/, ойр /ɔir̥/.
Schwa
The neutral vowel ə. Short vowels outside the first syllable become schwa, e.g. ахмад /axməd/.
Epenthetic vowel
A vowel with no phonemic weight that is inserted to break consonant clusters. The module treats non-initial short vowels this way (as in Svantesson's analysis).
Palatalization
Secondary raising of the tongue toward the hard palate, written ʲ, e.g. аль /aɮʲ/.
Iotated Vowel
The Cyrillic letters я, ю, ё, е, which stand for a glide j plus a vowel at the start of a syllable: юм /jʊm/.
Velar Nasal
The sound ŋ as in English "sing"; н is pronounced this way at the end of a word and before г, к, х: хүн /xuŋ/.
Vowel Harmony
The Mongolian system in which suffix vowels agree with the stem (back / front, rounded / unrounded). The transcription module does not use it to generate IPA; see Vowel Harmony.
Final Vowel Deletion
The module drops a word-final short vowel in words of two or more syllables: байна /bain/.
Devoiced r
The trill r becomes voiceless r̥ at the end of a word: сар /sar̥/.
Tie Bar
The mark ͡ joining the two parts of an affricate: t͡sʰ, d͡z, t͡ɕʰ, d͡ʒ.
Geminate
A doubled (long) consonant, written with ː: алла /aɮː/.
Syllable Nucleus
The vowel at the core of a syllable. The module splits words into syllables with mn.syllables() and treats the first one differently from the rest.

Interactive Features

  • Click words to cycle variants: Mongolian gives only one transcription per word, so clicking does not change anything.
  • Audio playback: Click the speaker icon next to any word or line to hear text-to-speech pronunciation (requires browser TTS support).
  • Export results: Use PDF or CSV buttons to save transcriptions in your preferred format.

Multiple Pronunciation Variants

For Mongolian each word has exactly one rule-generated output. There is no lexicon and no per-dialect variant, so the cycling feature available for German or Latin does not apply. If a word is transcribed incorrectly (see Common Issues), try an alternative spelling that reaches the intended rule.

Mongolian Pronunciation Guide

This guide explains how the module turns Cyrillic Mongolian into IPA. Spelling is fairly regular, but the Cyrillic letters hide a lot of structure: long vowels are written double, the vowel of any syllable after the first is reduced, and the final vowel of most words is not pronounced.

Initial vs Non-Initial Vowels

The most important rule in the module is that the first syllable keeps its full vowel quality and every short vowel in a later syllable is reduced to schwa ə (the exception is и, й, which stays i).

  • ном /nɔm/ keeps ɔ, but in модонд /mɔdənd/ the second о becomes ə.
  • ажил /ad͡ʒiɮ/ and багш /baɡɕ/: и is stable, and a consonant cluster without vowel stays as is.
  • Initial э and и merge: тэр /tʰir̥/ and тир /tʰir̥/ have the same vowel. Initial ү is u while у is ʊ: үг /uɡ/ vs ус /ʊs/.
  • Long vowels and diphthongs are exempt from reduction, wherever they occur: хаалга /xaːɮɡ/, наймаа /naimaː/.

Long Vowels and Diphthongs

Doubled vowel letters are long: аа, оо, уу, үү, өө, ээ. The letters ий and ии also give a long iː.

  • аа /aː/, оо /ɔː/, уу /ʊː/, үү /uː/, өө /ɵː/, ээ /eː/.
  • Long ээ is the only common source of eː: ширээ /ɕireː/, гэгээн /ɡiɡeːŋ/.
  • Diphthongs in й: ай /ai/, ой /ɔi/, уй /ʊi/, үй /ui/, өй /ɵi/, эй /ei/. Halh keeps these as diphthongs.
  • Rising diphthong-like sequences: ау /aʊ̯/, оа /ʊ̯a/, уа /u̯a/, ио /i̯oː/: ариун /ari̯ʊːŋ/, радио /radi̯oː/.
  • The doubled vowels are matched before single vowels inside each syllable, so long vowels are not reduced: даваа /daw̜aː/, долоо /dɔɮɔː/.

Iotated Vowels (я, ю, ё, е) and Palatalization

At the start of a word or after a vowel, the letters я, ю, ё, е are a glide plus a vowel. In the first syllable they give ja, jʊ, jɔ, jɵ; later in the word the vowel reduces to schwa (jə).

  • я /ja/, ю /jʊ/, ё /jɔ/, е /jɵ/.
  • ямар /jamər̥/, баяр /bajər̥/, хоёр /xɔjər̥/, ес /jɵs/.

After one of the consonants п, б, т, д, к, г, ф, в, х, м, н, р, л, the letters е, ю, ё, я do not give a glide. Instead the consonant is palatalized and the vowel is rewritten, before any other processing: е → ө, ю → у, ё → о, я → а. Hence:

  • бя /bʲa/, ня /nʲa/, ля /ɮʲa/, тю /tʰʲʊ/, дё /dʲɔ/.
  • Words with е after a consonant therefore get ɵ (a rounded vowel), e.g. Герман /ɡʲɵrməŋ/. This is a rule for Mongolian-like vowel harmony, not for Russian-style е (see Common Issues).

Palatalization after ж, з, с, ц, ч, ш, щ is not implemented (they are not in the palatalization table).

Stops: Aspiration, Not Voicing

Mongolian stops contrast aspiration, not voicing. The module renders the fortis letters as aspirated and the lenis letters with voiced symbols:

  • Aspirated: т → tʰ тав /tʰaw̜/, к → kʰ банк /baŋkʰ/, п → pʰ пүү /pʰuː/, ц and ч likewise.
  • Lenis: б → b бүх /bux/, д → d дав /daw̜/, г → ɡ гар /ɡar̥/.
  • Intervocalic voicing is not shown: the aspirated letters stay aspirated between vowels: акаа /akʰaː/, апаа /apʰaː/.

Affricates and Sibilants

  • ц → t͡sʰ цас /t͡sʰas/; з → d͡z зун /d͡zʊŋ/.
  • ч → t͡ɕʰ чих /t͡ɕʰix/; ж → d͡ʒ жил /d͡ʒiɮ/.
  • с → s сар /sar̥/; ш and щ → ɕ шар /ɕar̥/, щи /ɕi/.

Nasals: н, м, г and the Velar Nasal

  • н at the end of a word is ŋ: хүн /xuŋ/, сайн /saiŋ/.
  • н before г, к, х assimilates to ŋ: монгол /mɔŋɡəɮ/, банк /baŋkʰ/, мөнх /mɵŋx/.
  • н before б, п, м, ф assimilates to m: дэнбэ /dimp/; the cluster нб is written mp, as in улаанбаатар /ʊɮaːmpaːtʰər̥/.
  • Otherwise н stays n: нар /nar̥/, гэнэт /ɡinətʰ/.

Liquids: л and р

  • л is the voiced lateral fricative ɮ: улс /ʊɮs/, жил /d͡ʒiɮ/. The cluster лх is voiceless ɬʰ: лхагва /ɬʰaɡw̜/.
  • р is r inside the word: гарах /ɡarəx/. At the end of a word it is devoiced to r̥: сар /sar̥/, өөр /ɵːr̥/.
  • р before п, т, к, ц, ч is also voiceless: эрт /ir̥tʰ/, цирк /t͡sʰir̥kʰ/.

х, в, ф and щ

  • х → x in all positions: хот /xɔtʰ/, ах /ax/.
  • в → w̜: тав /tʰaw̜/, авах /aw̜əx/. It is the same symbol in all positions.
  • ф → f (loanwords): фото /fɔtʰ/.
  • щ is treated as ш: ɕ.

Geminates and Consonant Clusters

  • Doubled л, м, н, с are long: алла /aɮː/, аммаа /amːaː/, Анна /anː/, русс /rʊsː/.
  • Doubled п, т, к, г, ц, з, ч, ж, ф, в, ш, щ, х, р are simplified to a single consonant: аппа /apʰ/ (final vowel is deleted).
  • бб and вб become b: бб /b/, вб /b/.
  • дд is an unusual case: аддаа /at͉.taː/, Будда /bʊt͉.t/ (a geminate stop written with a syllable dot).
  • No vowel is inserted into clusters; the syllabifier treats permitted final clusters as a single syllable (see Implementation Details): багш /baɡɕ/, улс /ʊɮs/.

The Soft and Hard Signs (ь, ъ)

  • ь gives the palatalization mark ʲ on the preceding consonant: аль /aɮʲ/, арьс /arʲs/, сургууль /sʊrɡʊːɮʲ/, чинь /t͡ɕʰinʲ/.
  • ъ is silent and only separates syllables: объект /ɔbjəkʰtʰ/, подъезд /pʰɔdjəd͡zd/.
  • ью crashes the engine; see Common Issues.

Final Short Vowel Deletion

If the word has more than one syllable, a final short vowel is dropped (a code comment calls it "standard practice for 'n' stems etc."). The check is on the final character of the output, so it removes a, e, i, ɔ, ɵ, u, ʊ or ə (including the second part of ei). Long vowels are not touched. A final i is protected only after a, ɔ, u or ʊ (the diphthongs ai, ɔi, ui, ʊi).

  • байна /bain/, хаана /xaːn/, үнэ /un/, мөнгө /mɵŋɡ/, шинэ /ɕin/.
  • Preserved: ширээ /ɕireː/ (long), манай /manai/, арай /arai/ (diphthong ai).
  • эмэгтэй /iməɡtʰe/ loses the i of ei.
  • One-syllable words keep their vowel: чи /t͡ɕʰi/, ва /w̜a/.

Vowel Harmony

Mongolian vowel harmony (back / front, with rounding) is real and affects suffix spelling, but the IPA function export.to_IPA does not consult it. The helper mn.vowelharmony() in mn-common.lua exists for the Wiktionary inflection templates; the only time to_IPA reaches it is through the syllabifier when it meets ью (which is where the engine crashes). The vowel letter itself carries the harmony in Cyrillic, and the transcription simply maps each letter, so a word like гэр /ɡir̥/ and гар /ɡar̥/ differ only by their spelled vowel. Masculine/feminine/neutral classes are not computed anywhere in the output.

Stress

Following Svantesson (2005), word stress is treated as non-phonemic in Mongolian. The stress-marking code of the original Wiktionary module was removed, so no ˈ or ˌ marks are ever produced: монгол /mɔŋɡəɮ/.

Using the Tool: Practical Notes

Do I need to mark the stress?

No. Stress is treated as non-phonemic in Mongolian and the module produces no stress marks, so the input is plain Cyrillic spelling. See Stress.

Why do the vowels and the ending of a word differ from the spelling?

Short vowels after the first syllable are reduced to ə, and a final short vowel is deleted in words of more than one syllable: модонд /mɔdənd/, байна /bain/. Long vowels and diphthongs are exempt. See Initial vs Non-Initial Vowels and Final Short Vowel Deletion.

Does the tool use a dictionary, and why is there only one result?

No. Every word is generated by the rules alone, so each word has exactly one transcription and a spelling error is transcribed as typed. Only a phonemic form is offered; see Phonemic vs Phonetic.

Why is a word with ы or ью transcribed incorrectly?

The letter ы is excluded from the module's vowel pattern and passes through unchanged: газрын /ɡad͡zrыŋ/, whereas газрин /ɡad͡zriŋ/ is converted. The sequence ью makes the Lua call fail, and the input is returned unchanged. Russian loanwords are also affected by the palatalization rewrite. See Common Issues & Limitations.

Implementation Details (for Developers)

The whole pipeline is the single function export.to_IPA(text) in lua_modules/mn-pron_wasm.lua (about 85 lines). The JavaScript handler in utils.js calls it only for the Phonemic form: window.mn_ipa.to_IPA(cleanText). It uses the helper module mn-common.lua only for mn.syllables(), and mn/data.lua only for the table of permitted word-final clusters. The rest of mn-common.lua (vowel harmony, final vowel removal, the traditional-script variation selectors, the see and ipasee template entry points) is not used by to_IPA. The module's show() function is the Wiktionary template entry point and is not called by the app.

  1. Normalization: lowercase with mw.ustring.lower, delete every hyphen.
  2. Double consonant collapse: table double_consonants.
  3. Palatalization: table palatalisation.
  4. Syllabification: mn.syllables() splits the text.
  5. Per-syllable vowels: vowels3, vowels2, then vowels1 or vowels1_reduced.
  6. Consonant clusters: table consonants2.
  7. Single consonants: table consonants1.
  8. Contextual rules: nasal assimilation, r devoicing, final vowel deletion and cleanup.

Not Implemented

  • No stress (code removed), no allophonic voicing, no intervocalic spirantization of stops (the rule is present but inert for Cyrillic input as above; the Russian loanword spirantization rule is commented out), no vowel harmony computation, no lexicon, no phonetic form.
  • Russian-style palatalization after sibilants and affricates, and Russian e as e in loanwords, are not handled.
