About This Tool

Irish Dialects

Irish (Gaeilge) has three major dialect groups — Connacht, Munster, and Ulster — each with distinct phonological patterns. This tool supports all three dialects. Key dialectal differences include vowel quality (e.g., Connacht /aː/ vs Munster /æː/), consonant realization (e.g., word-final broad bh/mh → vˠ/vʲ in Connacht, weakened to w in some Ulster varieties), and stress placement (Munster attracts stress to sonorant-onset syllables). See the Wiktionary IPA/Irish module for the full reference.

This Irish transcription app uses a custom 17-pass phonological rule engine to generate IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) transcriptions for Irish text. Unlike other languages on this site, there is no Wiktionary pronunciation module for Irish — Irish IPA on Wiktionary is hand-entered per word. This engine provides automatic grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion for all three major dialects, making it the first browser-based automatic Irish IPA generator.

The engine uses a hybrid architecture: a 17-pass phonological rule pipeline based on Irish linguistics literature (Hickey 2014, Fuaimeanna na Gaeilge) plus a generated per-dialect lexical exception layer for known words. When a word is in the exception dictionary, the lexicon entry overrides the rule output for maximum accuracy. For unknown words, the rule-only output provides the best available generalization.

Dialects: Connacht, Munster & Ulster

Irish has three major spoken dialects, each with distinct phonological systems. This tool supports all three:

  • Connacht (primary target, most tuned): Spoken in counties Galway and Mayo. Features include anticipatory raising (coláiste → [kʊlˠaːʃtʲə]), broad l/n as denti-alveolar [l̪ˠ]/[n̪ˠ], and slender coda vowel gradation.
  • Munster (Southern, Kerry/Cork): Distinctive stress attraction to long vowels in non-initial syllables (cailín → [kaˈlʲiːnʲ]), éa-breaking ([eː] → [iːa̯]), epenthesis in obstruent+sonorant clusters, final -igh/-aigh → [ɪɟ], and verbal -f(e)adh → [əx].
  • Ulster (Northern, Donegal): Extensive post-tonic long-vowel shortening (scadán → [ˈsˠkad̪ˠənˠ]), a-fronting before slender consonants (baile → [ˈbˠælʲə]), o/u merger to [ʌ], ch-weakening [x]→[h], final vowel realizations (-adh→[u], -igh→[i]), and vowel nasalization before surviving mh.

Phonemic vs Phonetic

  • Phonemic: A broad transcription based on the rule pipeline + lexical exceptions. Represents the abstract phoneme system of the selected dialect.
  • Phonetic: Not currently available for Irish. The engine produces phonemic output with some allophonic detail (quality diacritics, stress placement).

Quick Reference (Glossary)

Broad / Slender (Caol / Leathan)
The fundamental distinction in Irish phonology. Consonants are marked as broad (velarized, ˠ) when adjacent to broad vowels (a, o, u) or slender (palatalized, ʲ) when adjacent to slender vowels (e, i). This distinction changes meaning: bó [bˠoː] (cow) vs beo [bʲoː] (alive).
Lenition (Séimhiú)
A grammatical mutation that turns stops into fricatives: b→bh [vˠ/vʲ], c→ch [x/ç], d→dh [ɣ/ɟ/j], g→gh [ɣ/ɟ/j], m→mh [w/vˠ/vʲ], p→ph [fˠ/fʲ], s→sh [h/ç], t→th [h/ç]. Marked by adding ⟨h⟩ after the consonant.
Eclipsis (Urú)
A grammatical mutation where voiced stops become nasals and voiceless stops become voiced: b→mb [m], c→gc [g], d→nd [n], f→bhf [w/vˠ], g→ng [ŋ], p→bp [b], t→dt [d]. Marked by a prefix.
Velarization (Broad)
A secondary articulation marked by ˠ, where the back of the tongue is raised toward the velum during consonant articulation. Gives a "dark" quality, noticeable on l [l̪ˠ] vs [lʲ].
Palatalization (Slender)
A secondary articulation marked by ʲ, where the tongue body is raised toward the hard palate during consonant articulation. Gives a "light" or "y-like" quality.
Dental Consonant
Broad coronal consonants (t, d, l, n) are articulated with the tongue against the upper teeth in Irish, marked with ̪: t̪ˠ, d̪ˠ, l̪ˠ, n̪ˠ.
Postalveolar Consonant
Slender coronal consonants (t, d, l, n) are articulated with the tongue blade against the postalveolar region in many dialects, marked with ̠: t̠ʲ, d̠ʲ, l̠ʲ, n̠ʲ.
Schwa
A neutral vowel sound ə, common in unstressed syllables — the default reduced vowel in Irish.
Fada (Síneadh Fada)
The acute accent over vowels (á, é, í, ó, ú) marking them as long. This is phonemic: cas [kasˠ] (turn) vs cás [kɑːsˠ] (case).
Diphthong
A gliding vowel sound where the tongue changes position during articulation. Irish has several from vowel+fricative vocalization: əu (from -abh/-amh), iə (from -ia-), uə (from -ua-).
Anticipatory Raising
A Connacht feature where short /a/ or /o/ in the first syllable raises to [ɪ]/[ʊ] when followed by a syllable with long [aː]: coláiste [kʊlˠaːʃtʲə], caisleán [kɪʃlʲɑːnˠ].
Svarabhakti (Epenthesis)
An epenthetic vowel [ə] inserted between heterorganic sonorant+obstruent clusters: dearg [ˈdʲaɾˠəɡ], gorm [ˈɡɔɾˠəmˠ]. In Munster, extends to obstruent+sonorant clusters: ocrach [ˈɔkɾˠəx] → [ˈɔkəɾˠəx].
Primary Stress
The main emphasis in a word (marked ˈ). Defaults to the first syllable in Connacht and Ulster, but attracted to long vowels in Munster.
Secondary Stress
Weaker emphasis (marked ˌ) on later syllables in long words or compounds.
Devoicing
Word-final devoicing of voiced stops: díobh → final [vʲ]→[fʲ]. Also applies regressively before certain suffixes.
Glide Insertion
Insertion of [j] or [w] between vowels in hiatus, or in Ulster word-final position after gh/dh vocalization: brúigh → [bˠɾˠuːj].

Irish Pronunciation Guide

This guide explains the fundamental rules of Irish pronunciation. Irish has a complex but largely regular orthography once the rules of broad/slender consonant quality, vowel length, stress, and mutation are understood.

Broad vs Slender (Caol vs Leathan)

The single most important concept in Irish phonology: every consonant has two varieties depending on the adjacent vowels. The rule is summarized: "caol le caol agus leathan le leathan" (slender with slender and broad with broad).

The Polarity Rule

  • Broad vowels: a, á, o, ó, u, ú — make adjacent consonants velarized [ˠ] and dental/coronal
  • Slender vowels: e, é, i, í — make adjacent consonants palatalized [ʲ] and postalveolar

Vowel Length & Quality

Irish distinguishes long and short vowels phonemically. Short vowels are lax; long vowels are tense.

Long Vowels

  • Fada-marked: á, é, í, ó, ú are always long (á → [ɑː], é → [eː], í → [iː], ó → [oː], ú → [uː])
  • Digraphs: Many vowel digraphs represent a single long vowel or diphthong: ao → [iː], eo → [oː]/[ɔː], ía → [iə], úa → [uə]
  • Vowel lengthening before geminates: Short vowels lengthen before heavy sonorant clusters in monosyllables: ceann → [cɑːn̪ˠ], poll → [pˠoːl̪ˠ]

Short Vowels

  • a → [a] (front), o → [ɔ], u → [ʊ], i → [ɪ], e → [ɛ]
  • Unstressed reduction: Unstressed short vowels reduce to schwa [ə]
  • Vowel gradation: Short vowels can raise/lower depending on coda consonant quality (Connacht: broad coda keeps [a], slender coda raises to [ɛ])

Stress Patterns

Irish stress patterns vary dramatically by dialect — this is one of the most distinctive differences.

Connacht & Ulster: Initial Stress

  • Default rule: Primary stress falls on the first syllable: cailín [ˈkalʲiːnʲ], Gaeltacht [ˈɡeːl̪ˠt̪ˠəxt̪ˠ]
  • Compounds: First component carries primary stress: Gaeltacht [ˈɡeːl̪ˠt̪ˠəxt̪ˠ], deirfiúr [ˈdʲɛɾʲəfʲuːɾʲ]
  • Second syllable stress: A small set of a-prefixed adverbs take second-syllable stress: arís [əˈɾʲiːʃ], amach [əˈmˠax]

Munster: Stress Attraction

Munster stress is attracted to the first long vowel or diphthong after the first syllable (Hickey II.3, Ó Sé):

  • Long vowel in σ₂: Stress moves to σ₂ — cailín [kaˈlʲiːnʲ], Tomáisín [t̪ˠəˌmˠɑːˈʃiːnʲ]
  • Short + short + long (σ₃): Stress lands on σ₃ — ceannasaí [canˠəˈsˠiː]
  • -(e)acht attraction: Short σ₁ + -(e)acht in σ₂ attracts stress unless the σ₂ onset is a sonorant (r/l/n/h): portach [pəɾˠˈt̪ˠɑx] vs ocrach [ˈɔkɾˠəx]
  • Verbal resistance: Synthetic verb endings resist attraction: freastalaím [ˈfʲɾʲasˠt̪ˠəlˠiːmʲ]

Lenition Realizations

  • After vowels: bh/mh weaken to [w] (or [vˠ]/[vʲ] if sustained): leabhar [l̠ʲauɾˠ], scríobh [ʃcɾʲiːw]
  • Intervocalic dh/gh: Silent or [j]/[ɣ]: fidheall [ˈfʲiːəl̪ˠ], laghad [ˈl̪ˠəid̪ˠ]
  • Word-final th: Silent after short vowels: rath [ɾˠa], both [bˠɔ]
  • fh-: Always silent: fhuil [wɪlʲ], fhiacail [ˈiəkəlʲ]

Eclipsis (Urú)

Eclipsis is triggered by possessive adjectives (ár, bhur, a), numbers (seacht, ocht, naoi, deich), and certain prepositions. The eclipsing consonant is a prefix that replaces or modifies the original sound:

T-Prefix Mutation (not eclipsis)

Word-initial ts- and tch- are T-prefix mutations (Hickey III.2.2.2), not eclipsis. The first consonant survives while the second is silenced: ts→t, tch→t (e.g. túis → [t̪ˠuːʃ]).

Consonant Clusters

Irish simplifies complex initial and medial clusters through phonological rules:

Initial Cluster Simplification

  • cn→cr, gn→gr, mn→mr, tn→tr: The non-coronal element survives and a liquid appears: cnoc → [kɾˠɔk], gnáth → [ɡɾˠɑːh]
  • ts- / tch-: T-prefix mutation → first consonant survives, second silenced: tsúil → [t̪ˠuːlʲ], tsean → [t̠ʲanˠ]

Medial Clusters

  • s+stop heterosyllabic: s closes the preceding syllable, stop starts the next: aistriú → [aʃ.ˈtʲɾʲuː]
  • Sonorant+obstruent: May trigger epenthesis (see below)
  • n→ŋ before velar stops: seanchaí → [ˈʃanˠxiː] but slán go fóill → [sˠl̪ˠɑːŋ ɡə fˠoːlʲ]

Epenthesis (Svarabhakti)

Irish inserts a short epenthetic vowel [ə] (or [ɪ] in some contexts) between consonants in certain clusters:

Connacht & Ulster

  • Heterorganic sonorant+obstruent: Insert [ə] when the preceding vowel is short and stressed: dearg [ˈdʲaɾˠəɡ], gorm [ˈɡɔɾˠəmˠ], seilg [ˈʃɛlʲəɟ]
  • Exclusions: Homorganic clusters (rd, rl, rn, nd, ld, nn, ll, rr) do not epenthesize
  • Lexical exceptions: -airg, -eirg → [ɪ] not [ə]: tairg [ˈt̪ˠaɾʲɪɟ]

Munster (Extended Epenthesis)

Munster extends epenthesis to obstruent+sonorant clusters after the stressed syllable (Ó Sé):

  • m+n: feamnach [ˈfʲamˠən̪ˠəx]
  • g+l: eagla [ˈaɡəl̪ˠə]
  • d+r: eadrainn [ˈad̪ˠəɾˠən̠ʲ]
  • th+r: cathrach [ˈkahəɾˠəx]
  • ch+r: achrann [ˈaxəɾˠən̪ˠ]
  • s+r: lasrach [ˈl̪ˠɑsˠəɾˠəx]
  • th+n: aithne [ˈahənʲə]
  • Also applies in pre-stress position when stress attraction moves stress off the cluster's syllable: Colmán [kəl̪ˠəˈmˠɑːn̪ˠ], purgóid [pˠəɾˠəˈɡoːdʲ]

Sonorants: L, N, R

Irish has a famously complex sonorant system with four-way l/n distinctions and three-way r distinctions:

L and N

Context Broad l Slender l Broad n Slender n
Before consonant (coda) l̪ˠ l̠ʲ n̪ˠ n̠ʲ
Intervocalic / initial l̪ˠ lʲ n̪ˠ nʲ
Geminate (ll, nn) l̪ˠ l̠ʲ n̪ˠ n̠ʲ

R

  • Broad r: Alveolar tap [ɾˠ] — rá [ɾˠɑː], carraig [ˈkaɾˠəɟ]
  • Slender r: Palatalized tap [ɾʲ] — rí [ɾʲiː], tirim [ˈtʲɪɾʲəmʲ]
  • Geminate rr: Broad only [ɾˠ] (lengthening effect on preceding vowel) — carraig [ˈkaɾˠəɟ]

Vowel Lengthening Before Sonorants

Short vowels lengthen in monosyllables before geminate sonorants (ll, nn, rr, mm) and heavy clusters (rd, rl, rn):

  • ceann [cɑːn̪ˠ], poll [pˠoːl̪ˠ], corr [koːɾˠ]
  • ard [ɑːɾˠd̪ˠ], bord [bˠoːɾˠd̪ˠ], tarna [ˈt̪ˠɑːɾˠn̪ˠə]

Word-Final Codas

Several phonological processes apply at the end of words:

  • -adh vocalization: [ə] or [uː] (Ulster: [u]) — margadh [ˈmˠaɾˠəɡuː] (Connacht)
  • -igh/-aigh: [iː] or [i] — ceannaigh [ˈcanˠiː] (Connacht), Munster: [ɪɟ]
  • -th deletion: After short vowels — rath [ɾˠa], both [bˠɔ]
  • bh/mh→w: After long vowels — scríobh [ʃcɾʲiːw], sníomh [ʃnʲiːw]
  • Ulster final -ach→[ah]: Éireannach [ˈeːɾʲən̪ˠah]
  • Munster final -adh→[əx]: d'fhágadh [ˈd̪ˠɑːɡəx]
  • Ulster j-glide: Final long vowel + gh/dh → [j] offglide: brúigh [bˠɾˠuːj]

Dialect: Connacht Features

Connacht is the primary, most-tuned dialect. Key phonological rules in the engine:

  • Anticipatory raising (pass 06d): Short /a/→[ɪ], /o/→[ʊ] when the second syllable has long [aː]: coláiste → [kʊlˠˈaːʃtʲə], caisleán → [kɪʃlʲˈɑːnˠ]
  • Vowel gradation (pass 08): Short vowels before a coda consonant raise/lower based on the coda's quality: slender coda → [ɪ]/[ɛ], broad coda → [a]/[ɔ]
  • Broad l/n → denti-alveolar (pass 13): Dental diacritic [̪] in coda position and after stops
  • -íocht suffix: [iːçtʲ] → [iəxt̪ˠ]: ríocht [ɾʲiəxt̪ˠ], draíocht [d̪ˠɾˠiːəxt̪ˠ]
  • w→vˠ after long vowel: Broad mh coda retains friction: snámh [sˠn̪ˠɑːvˠ]
  • Function word reduction: do→[ɡə], is→[sˠ], agam→[uɡəmˠ]

Dialect: Munster Features

  • Stress attraction (pass 02): Stress moves to the first long vowel after the first syllable. Exception: verbal synthetic endings resist attraction.
  • éa-breaking (pass 15): [eː] → [iːa̯] under stress (méar [mʲiːa̯ɾˠ]), [ia̯] pretonic (éadóchas [ia̯ˈd̪ˠoːxəsˠ]). Only first-syllable éa breaks.
  • -igh/-aigh → [ɪɟ] (not [iː] as in Connacht/Ulster): ceannaigh [ˈcan̪ˠɪɟ], balaigh [ˈbˠɑlˠɪɟ]
  • Extended epenthesis (pass 12/15): Obst+son clusters get [ə] even in unstressed syllables
  • Verbal -f(e)adh → [əx]: d'fhágadh [ˈd̪ˠɑːɡəx], beireadh [ˈbʲɛɾʲəx]
  • Final -th → [h] (not silent): tláith [t̪ˠl̪ˠɑːh], dlúth [d̪ˠl̪ˠuːh]
  • Geminate diphthongization (pass 13): Short vowel + geminate in closed syllable breaks to diphthong: fonn [fˠɑun̪ˠ], tonn [t̪ˠɑun̪ˠ]
  • o before nasal → [ɔ] (not [ʊ]): sona [ˈsˠɔn̪ˠə], long [l̪ˠɔŋ]
  • First-syllable ai → [a] (not [ai])
  • Sonorant notation: Broad l/n → dental [l̪ˠ]/[n̪ˠ]; slender l/n → plain [lʲ]/[nʲ] (not postalveolar as in Connacht/Ulster). ~73 lexical exceptions

Dialect: Ulster Features

  • Post-tonic long-vowel shortening (pass 11): Long vowels after the stressed syllable shorten: scadán [ˈsˠkad̪ˠənˠ], dochtúir [ˈd̪ˠɔxt̪ˠuɾʲ]. ~197 lexical exceptions resist shortening.
  • Short o/u merger → [ʌ] (Hickey I.2.3): sop [sˠʌpˠ], cur [kʌɾˠ]. Lexical set of ~100 words keeps [ɔ].
  • á→aː (not [ɑː] as in Connacht): cás [kaːsˠ]. Post-tonic á fronts to [æ] before slender consonants: úsáid [uːsˠædʲ], ofráil [ɔfˠɾˠælʲ]
  • a→æ before slender consonant (pass 15): baile [ˈbˠælʲə], maide [ˈmˠædʲə]. ~55 lexical exceptions keep [a] (taibhse, caipín, aimsir).
  • ó→ɔː (not [oː]): bróg [bˠɾˠɔːɡ], mór [mˠɔːɾˠ]
  • -ach→[ah] (weakening [x]→[h]): Éireannach [ˈeːɾʲən̪ˠah]. Lexical set of ~77 words.
  • Final -adh→[u] (short, not [uː]): ghearradh [ˈjaɾˠu], canadh [ˈkanˠu]. ~33 lexical exceptions keep long [uː] (glacadh, margadh).
  • Final -igh/-aigh→[i] (short, not [iː]): ceannaigh [ˈcanˠi]. ~12 exceptions keep [iː] (bradaigh, fiafraigh).
  • Final -ach→[a] (not schwa): casach [ˈkasˠa], bocht [bˠʌxt̪ˠ]
  • bh/mh diphthong → [au] (not [əu]): amhras [ˈauɾˠəsˠ], leabhar [l̠ʲauɾˠ]. [au]→[oː] before r: tabhair [t̪ˠoːɾʲ] (22 vs 4 keepers).
  • Vowel nasalization before surviving mh: nimhe [n̠ʲɨ̃vʲə], crumhóg [kɾˠʊ̃wɔɡ]. Lexical set of ~13 words.
  • j-glide after final long vowel from gh/dh: brúigh [bˠɾˠuːj], cruaidh [kɾˠuəj]. Only u/a-vocalic roots; iː-final excluded.
  • rs-leveling: [ɾˠ]+[ʃ] → [ɾˠ]+[sˠ]: tuirseach [ˈt̪ˠʌɾˠsˠax]
  • Verbal -im → broad [mˠ]: éistim [ˈeːʃtʲəmˠ] (including -ím words with strip_fadas)

Implementation Details (for Developers)

The engine is implemented as a 17-pass token-array pipeline in Lua. Each pass receives the token array and context, modifies tokens in place (or replaces the array), and returns it.

  1. Polarity (pass 01): Determines broad/slender quality from flanking vowels. Simplifies initial clusters (cn→cr, gn→gr, mn→mr, tn→tr).
  2. Stress (pass 02): Primary stress on first syllable (Connacht/Ulster) or attracted to long vowel (Munster). Computes syllable count.
  3. Eclipsis (pass 03): Word-initial mutation clusters (mb→m, gc→g, dt→d, bp→b, bhf→w, ng→ŋ, nn→n). T-prefix silencing (ts→t, tch→t).
  4. Cluster simplification (pass 04): Merges adjacent consonants from compound clusters.
  5. Mutated fricatives (pass 05): Lenited fricative realizations after vowels.
  6. Vocalization (pass 06): Vowel+fricative sequences → diphthongs/long vowels.
  7. Anticipatory raising (pass 06d): Connacht-only /a/→[ɪ], /o/→[ʊ] before long [aː].
  8. Nasalization (pass 07): o/u/ó/ú → [uː] before geminate nasals.
  9. Slender coda (pass 08): Vowel gradation before slender ng/nn.
  10. Consonants (pass 09): Resolves all consonant tokens to IPA. Lenition, quality, clusters.
  11. Vowel adjunct (pass 09b): Resolves vowel + mutated fricative sequences.
  12. Vowels (pass 10): Dialect-specific vowel resolution with allophonic rules.
  13. Unstressed reduction (pass 11): Reduces unstressed short vowels to [ə]. Epicene exceptions.
  14. Epenthesis (pass 12): Svarabhakti vowel [ə] in sonorant+obstruent clusters.
  15. Sonorants (pass 13): 4-way l/n diacritic system; lengthening before geminates.
  16. Final cleanup (pass 14): Silent fricatives, devoicing, j-glides, function word overrides, multi-word sandhi, -íocht override.
  17. Dialect finalize (pass 15): Per-dialect surface normalizations (Ulster aː→æ, Munster éa-breaking, sonorant flatten, etc.) — last pass, immune to later overwrites.

Lexical Exception Layer

In addition to the rule pipeline, the engine has a generated per-dialect exception dictionary (passes/lex_subs_<dialect>.lua) applied after rendering. This standard hybrid G2P design covers ~1166 Connacht, ~1524 Munster, and ~2049 Ulster words that the rules cannot derive. The exception layer is regenerated after every major rule change to only cover words that genuinely need per-word treatment.
